What to Do If Your Google Business Profile Attributes Are Incorrect or Misleading

GrowGigs Team

Attributes are the small checkboxes on your Google Business Profile, wheelchair accessible, woman-owned, outdoor seating, appointment-only, that let customers filter and judge your listing before they ever read a single review. When one of these is wrong, outdated, or simply misleading, it can quietly cost you customers who filtered you out, or worse, send you customers expecting something you don't actually offer.

Why attributes matter more than most owners realize

Customers increasingly use Google's attribute filters, searching specifically for "wheelchair accessible" or "accepts walk-ins," before a business ever appears in their results at all. An incorrect attribute doesn't just look sloppy on your profile, it can remove you entirely from a filtered search a genuinely interested customer is actively running, or pull in customers who show up expecting an accommodation or service you don't actually provide.

Where incorrect attributes typically come from

  • Attributes Google infers automatically from signals like photos, reviews, or your category selection, sometimes inaccurately, without you ever actively choosing them.
  • Outdated attributes you set once and never revisited, accurate when your business opened but no longer true after a renovation, ownership change, or service shift.
  • Attributes suggested by users through Google's "Suggest an Edit" feature, which can introduce inaccuracies if nobody from your business catches and corrects them.

How to actually check and fix them

Log into your Google Business Profile manager and review the full attributes list under your profile's info section, not just the ones Google happens to surface prominently, since several categories of attributes exist and not all of them get equal visibility in the default view. Uncheck anything inaccurate and add anything genuinely true but currently missing, since an incomplete profile loses filterable visibility just as much as an inaccurate one does.

Ownership and identity attributes deserve particular care

Attributes like "veteran-owned," "women-led," or "LGBTQ+ friendly" carry real weight with customers actively seeking out that kind of business, and claiming one inaccurately, even unintentionally, is both a trust problem if discovered and occasionally subject to platform verification if challenged. Only select these when they're genuinely and currently true, not because they seem likely to attract more clicks.

Accessibility attributes carry a different kind of risk

Marking a location as wheelchair accessible when it genuinely isn't creates a real, practical problem for an actual customer who shows up expecting that accommodation, not just a reputational one. Verify physical accessibility attributes against the actual current state of your location rather than assuming details from years ago still hold, especially after any renovation or relocation. A customer who physically cannot use your entrance after being told online that they could is a far worse outcome than simply losing a click from someone who filtered you out correctly.

Google-suggested edits need active monitoring

Because other users can suggest attribute edits to your profile, similar to the mechanics covered in how profile changes can trigger a suspension review, periodically check what's actually live on your listing rather than assuming it still matches what you originally set. A suggested edit that goes unreviewed can sit live on your profile for a surprisingly long time before anyone from your business notices.

Treat attribute accuracy as ongoing maintenance

Add a quick attributes review to whatever regular cadence you already use for checking your profile's Q&A section or other listing details, rather than treating it as a one-time setup task completed when you first claimed your profile. A business that changes its hours, services, or accessibility over time needs its attributes to keep pace with those changes. Assign this check to someone specific on your team, since a task with no clear owner tends to get skipped entirely once the initial profile setup excitement wears off.
