Can a Negative Review Be Removed If the Reviewer Privately Apologizes or Retracts the Complaint?

GrowGigs Team

A customer leaves a harsh one-star review, you reach out privately to make it right, and they reply saying they're sorry, the review was unfair, they'll take care of it. Then nothing happens, the review just sits there, unchanged, while you're left wondering what a private apology actually accomplishes toward getting it removed.

A private apology, by itself, does nothing to the review

Google, Trustpilot, and every comparable platform require the reviewer themselves to take the actual action, editing or deleting their own review, no business, however sincere the private exchange, can delete or edit someone else's review on their behalf. A screenshot of an apologetic message, however genuine, isn't something you can submit to a platform and expect to result in removal, since it doesn't change the review's actual status on the platform at all.

Why this is different from reporting a policy violation

A private apology isn't evidence of a policy violation, it's evidence the reviewer regrets what they posted, which is an entirely different thing from a reportable violation under the platform's actual content rules. Platforms remove content based on policy violations, not based on a reviewer's later change of heart, so a heartfelt private apology, however useful it feels in the moment, doesn't translate into grounds for a report.

What actually has to happen next

  • Ask the reviewer directly to update or remove it themselves, a polite, specific request, pointing them to exactly where to find their own review, is more effective than waiting for them to figure it out on their own.
  • Offer to help them locate it, many people genuinely don't know how to find or edit a review they posted, especially on a platform they don't use often, so a short, clear set of steps increases the odds they'll follow through.
  • Follow up once, reasonably, people who sincerely intend to update a review sometimes just forget, but repeated requests cross into pressure that can read poorly if the exchange ever became public.

Get something in writing before you count on it

If the apology happened verbally or over a call, ask for a quick written follow-up, a text or email confirming they'll update the review, both so you have something concrete and so the request itself prompts them to actually follow through rather than letting the conversation fade. This isn't about building a legal case, it's simply that a verbal-only exchange is the easiest kind of commitment to let slip.

Don't let the apology substitute for your own public response

While you're waiting to see whether the reviewer follows through, the review is still live and visible to every other customer reading it, so a thoughtful public response is still worth posting in the meantime. Remove or soften that public response later if the review does get updated, but don't leave the negative review unaddressed publicly just because a private resolution seems to be in progress.

If they never follow through, you're not stuck

A reviewer who apologized but never actually updated the review hasn't left you without options, the review still gets evaluated on its own merits for any genuine policy violation, and a solid public response still does real work regardless of what happened privately. Treat the private apology as a possible bonus outcome, not the plan itself, since the plan has to work whether or not that follow-through ever actually happens.

Keep the private exchange itself off the public record

Resist the temptation to reference the private apology in your public response to the review, quoting or paraphrasing a private message in a public reply can feel vindicating in the moment but tends to read poorly to other customers and can understandably make the reviewer feel exposed after a conversation they believed was private. Keep the public response focused on the facts of the original complaint, and keep whatever happened in the private exchange exactly where it happened, privately.
