Why Did Google Reject My Review Removal Request?

GrowGigs Team

A rejection isn't necessarily the end of the road, but resubmitting the identical report and expecting a different result usually is a dead end. Here are the reasons rejections actually happen, and what's worth doing about each one.

You picked the wrong policy category

Google asks you to select a reason when you flag a review (spam, fake engagement, conflict of interest, and so on). Picking the closest-sounding option instead of the one that actually applies is one of the most common reasons a legitimate case still gets rejected, the system is checking against the specific category you chose, not the general spirit of your complaint.

The evidence didn't support the claim

Saying a review is fake isn't evidence that it's fake. Rejections often come down to a report that made an assertion without anything backing it, no reviewer history, no timeline, no explanation of how you know the reviewer was never a customer. A reviewer's account showing a pattern of similar reviews on unrelated businesses is the kind of detail that actually moves a case.

The review technically doesn't violate policy

Sometimes the rejection is correct. A harsh but accurate account of a real experience, even one you strongly disagree with, isn't a policy violation just because it's damaging. This is the hardest rejection to accept, but it's also the most common, and no amount of resubmitting changes it.

It went through automated review only

Borderline cases that don't clearly match a known pattern can get an automated rejection without ever reaching a human reviewer. This is where escalating through Business Profile support, citing the specific policy again with your evidence, can actually get a different outcome than an identical resubmission would.

What to actually do after a rejection

  • Re-read the rejection reason. If Google states which policy it didn't find a violation of, that tells you exactly what's missing.
  • Add evidence, don't just resubmit. Screenshots of the reviewer's account history, patterns across other businesses, or timing details.
  • Escalate through support once, with specifics. Reference the exact policy and evidence, not a general "please review again."
  • Know when to stop. If the review is genuine and simply negative, further attempts are unlikely to succeed and can read as harassment of the platform's process.

The honest bottom line

A rejection is information, not necessarily a final answer. The move is to figure out which of these four reasons applies to your case, then either strengthen the report or accept that this particular review doesn't qualify.
