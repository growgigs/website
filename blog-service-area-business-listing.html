How to Handle a Service-Area Business Listing Without a Storefront

GrowGigs Team

A plumber, mobile detailer, or home-cleaning crew that works out of a van and a garage faces a Google Business Profile setup problem that a retail storefront never has to think about: what address do you even put on the listing?

What a service-area business actually is, in Google's terms

Google has a specific designation for this: a service-area business (SAB) is one that travels to customers rather than serving them at a fixed location. Plumbers, electricians, cleaners, tutors, mobile mechanics, and locksmiths are the classic examples. The setup is meaningfully different from a standard Google Business Profile optimization approach built around a visible storefront.

Hide your address, don't fake one

The correct setup is to list your real business address during verification but set the profile to hide it from public view, then define your service areas by city, zip code, or region instead. What you should never do is list a fake commercial address just to appear more established, that's a direct guideline violation and a common reason a profile gets suspended after a competitor or Google's own systems flag it.

Define service areas realistically

  • Don't claim the whole state. Google allows up to 20 service areas, but listing an unrealistically large radius you can't actually service dilutes relevance and can read as spam to Google's systems.
  • Center it on where you actually work. List the specific cities or zip codes you genuinely serve regularly, not the farthest edge case job you've ever taken.
  • Keep it consistent with your website and ads. If your site, your ad targeting, and your profile all describe different service areas, that inconsistency is itself a weak local SEO signal.

NAP consistency still matters, even without a public address

Hiding your address from public view on the profile doesn't exempt you from name-address-phone consistency across directories and citations, Google still uses your verified address internally to determine proximity and relevance for local search, it's just not shown to searchers. A mismatched phone number across your website, your ads, and directory listings is just as damaging for an SAB as it is for a storefront, arguably more so, since the phone number is often the only consistent identifier a searcher has to go on.

Reviews work the same, with one practical wrinkle

Review collection and management for an SAB work identically to any other profile, but since customers never visit a physical location, the natural prompts, a receipt on the counter, a sign by the register, don't exist. The ask has to happen through the job itself: a text link after the appointment, a card left with the invoice, or a follow-up email are the realistic equivalents.

Categories matter more without a visible location

Without a storefront for a searcher to glance at and infer what you do, your primary and secondary category selection carries more weight in signaling exactly what service you offer and to whom, since there's no physical cue to fall back on.

Multiple SAB profiles for the same owner

Owners running service businesses under one brand across several metro areas sometimes wonder whether separate profiles per region help or hurt. Where the service area genuinely differs and operations are distinct enough to justify it, Google permits this, but it needs real separation in practice, not just a duplicated listing to appear in more city searches, which risks the same problems that affect single-location businesses running extra profiles for the same address.

Verification can take longer for a mobile business

Google sometimes requests additional documentation, licenses, insurance certificates, or a video verification call, before approving an SAB profile, specifically because there's no physical location for an automated or postcard-based check to confirm. Expect this step to take longer than a standard storefront verification, and have the paperwork ready before you start rather than scrambling once Google asks.

What this changes about day-to-day management

Since there's no walk-in foot traffic, posts, Q&A responses, and photo updates on an SAB profile carry more relative weight; they're often the only ongoing signal of activity a searcher and Google's algorithm see, making consistent profile upkeep more important, not less, than it is for a business with a physical location to anchor trust.
