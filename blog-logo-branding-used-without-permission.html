What to Do If Your Business Logo or Branding Is Being Used Without Permission

GrowGigs Team

A logo or brand identity being used somewhere it shouldn't be is a different kind of problem from copied website text, it usually touches trademark rights, not just copyright. Here's how to approach it.

Why this is a trademark issue, not just a copyright one

Copyright protects the specific creative work, the artwork of your logo itself. Trademark protects your brand identity, the logo, name, and visual identity as a source identifier that customers associate with your business. Unauthorized use of your logo can raise both, but the trademark angle is usually the more relevant one when the core issue is confusion about who's actually behind what's being presented.

What actually matters: likelihood of confusion

The central question in most logo misuse cases isn't just "did they use it without asking", it's whether the use is likely to confuse customers about the source of a product or service. A competitor using a strikingly similar logo in the same industry creates real confusion risk. A fan account, a parody, or a clearly non-commercial reference might use your branding without creating that same risk, which changes how straightforward a case is.

Document before you act

Screenshot the misuse with the URL, date, and context visible. If it's on a physical product or storefront, photograph it. If you can find when it first appeared, note that too. This record matters whether you end up sending a direct request, filing a platform report, or eventually involving an attorney.

Start with a direct request where possible

If you can identify who's behind the misuse, a direct, documented request to stop is often the fastest path. Many instances of logo misuse are more careless than malicious, a reseller using your logo without a licensing agreement, a former partner who never took it down after the relationship ended. A clear, professional request resolves a meaningful share of these without needing to escalate further.

Platform reporting for online misuse

Most platforms, social media, marketplaces, website hosts, have dedicated trademark or brand-abuse reporting channels separate from general copyright takedown forms. These typically ask for your registered trademark information if you have it, though even an unregistered but established brand identity can sometimes be reported depending on the platform's specific policy. Look specifically for the platform's trademark or intellectual property reporting form rather than its general content-complaint channel, filing through the right one tends to route the report to reviewers equipped to evaluate it properly.

Does it matter if your logo is registered as a trademark

A registered trademark generally gives you stronger, more clearly documented rights and a faster path through most platform reporting systems, which often have a dedicated field for a registration number. An unregistered logo can still carry real rights in many jurisdictions if you've been using it in commerce and it's become associated with your business, but proving that association takes more work than pointing to a registration certificate. If brand protection is an ongoing concern rather than a one-time issue, registration is worth looking into beyond just this specific instance.

When it's worth involving an attorney

Clear commercial confusion, a competitor using a near-identical logo to attract your customers, a scam operation impersonating your brand, or a case where direct requests and platform reports haven't worked, are situations where formal trademark enforcement becomes worth the cost. For a one-off, low-stakes instance of misuse, that level of escalation is rarely necessary.
