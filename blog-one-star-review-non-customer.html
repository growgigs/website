How to Handle a One-Star Review From Someone Who Was Never a Customer

GrowGigs Team

A one-star review from someone who never actually bought from you or set foot in your business is a direct policy violation, Google requires reviews to reflect a genuine experience. The catch is that "I know they were never a customer" isn't evidence on its own. Here's what actually is.

Why this category is worth pursuing

Unlike a genuine negative review, which Google's system is built to protect, a review from a non-customer has no legitimate basis to begin with. It doesn't matter how the review reads, if the person never transacted with or visited your business, it shouldn't be there. The work is in proving it.

Evidence that actually helps

  • No matching record. Check your booking system, POS, CRM, or customer list for the reviewer's name. No match is a starting point, not proof on its own, since customers don't always use their real name.
  • Location or service area mismatch. A review citing a location, service, or product you don't actually offer is a strong signal.
  • Timing that doesn't add up. A review posted for a date you were closed, or before you opened, or during a period with no matching transaction.
  • The reviewer's own account history. A profile with a pattern of one-off negative reviews across unrelated, sometimes competing, businesses in your area is a common sign this isn't a real customer relationship.
  • Direct admission. Occasionally the review itself gives it away, referencing a competitor, or language that makes clear they didn't actually visit ("I heard from a friend that...").

How to file the report

Flag the review through your Google Business Profile and select the reason that matches, typically the option related to reviews not based on a genuine experience. Include what you found: no matching customer record, the specific mismatch (date, location, service), and anything unusual in the reviewer's account history. A report that lays this out clearly moves faster than one that just asserts "this is fake."

If it's a competitor

If your evidence points to a competitor or someone connected to one, that's a separate, even stronger category, conflict of interest. Note that specifically in your report if you have reasonable grounds to believe it, since it's a distinct policy violation from a general non-customer review.

What to expect

Non-customer reviews with solid supporting evidence are among the more reliably successful removal categories, but "reliably successful" isn't "guaranteed", Google still makes the call case by case. A well-documented report with two or three of the evidence types above gives you the strongest shot.
