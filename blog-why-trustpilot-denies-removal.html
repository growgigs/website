Why Trustpilot Might Deny Your Review Removal Request

GrowGigs Team

Not every flagged review on Trustpilot comes down, and the decline reasons tend to follow a pattern. Here's what typically causes a rejection and what's actually worth doing about it.

The reviewer substantiated their experience

When a review is flagged as not based on a genuine experience, Trustpilot gives the reviewer a window to respond with proof. If they come back with something that holds up, an order number, a booking reference, a receipt that matches your own records, the review generally stays. This is the most common reason a "no genuine experience" flag gets declined: the case assumed silence, and the reviewer didn't stay silent.

The violation wasn't clear-cut

Trustpilot's guidelines cover real categories (abusive content, personal data, conflict of interest, off-topic complaints) but applying them requires judgment calls. A review that's harsh, unfair in tone, or paints your business unflatteringly isn't automatically a policy violation, Trustpilot's role is enforcing its guidelines, not acting as a referee on whether a complaint seems reasonable. If a report leans on "this isn't fair" rather than a specific guideline the review actually breaks, it's more likely to be declined.

The evidence didn't tie back to your own records

A report built on "we don't believe this is a real customer" without anything behind it carries far less weight than a direct records check. What moves a case is something concrete: a search of your order system, CRM, or booking platform that turns up no match for the name, email, or transaction the review references, not just a stated hunch that something's off.

The review is critical but accurate

If the review reflects a real interaction, even one that went badly, no amount of resubmission changes that. Trustpilot is explicit that genuine feedback, including sharply worded feedback, stays up by design. This is the hardest category to accept, but it's also one of the most common reasons a well-documented report still comes back declined.

What separates an accepted case from a declined one

Looking at the pattern in reverse is often more useful than the decline reasons on their own. Cases that go through tend to share a few things: the grounds cited match an actual policy category rather than a general sense that the review is unfair, the evidence is a direct records check rather than a stated belief, and the report is filed promptly rather than after the reviewer's response window has already closed. None of that guarantees an outcome, but it's the difference between a report that gives Trustpilot something concrete to act on and one that asks them to take your word for it.

What's actually worth doing after a decline

Trustpilot typically explains its reasoning when it declines a report. If that reasoning points to something fixable, more specific evidence, a corrected policy category, a missing piece of documentation, it's worth resubmitting with the gap closed. If the decline cites that the reviewer substantiated their experience, or that the content reflects a genuine interaction, repeating the same report unchanged won't produce a different outcome. At that point, a thoughtful public response addressing the complaint usually does more for your profile than continuing to contest it, and it signals to anyone else reading the review that you took it seriously rather than ignoring it.

Why this matters more than it might seem

A declined request isn't just a dead end, it's information. It tells you whether the problem is the evidence, the category, or the underlying fact that the review is genuine. Businesses that treat every decline as "the system failed" tend to keep resubmitting the same weak report and getting the same result. Businesses that read the decline reason and adjust accordingly end up either with a successful second attempt or a clear, early signal to stop contesting and respond publicly instead, both better outcomes than an open-ended back and forth.

The honest pattern

Most declines aren't arbitrary. They tend to fall into one of the categories above, and figuring out which one applies to your case is the difference between a resubmission worth the effort and one that's just going to come back declined again.
