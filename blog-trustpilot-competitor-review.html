Can You Remove a Trustpilot Review Posted by a Competitor?

GrowGigs Team

A harsh one-star review from an account with no real purchase history, posted right after a competitor launched nearby, raises an obvious question. Trustpilot does have a category for exactly this situation, but proving it takes more than suspicion.

Conflict of interest is a real, named category

Trustpilot's guidelines explicitly cover reviews posted by someone with a conflict of interest, including a competitor, a current or former employee of a competitor, or anyone with a commercial stake in harming your rating. This is a distinct category from the general "not based on a genuine experience" flag, and it's worth citing specifically if you believe it applies.

Suspicion alone doesn't move a case

Businesses often suspect a competitor is behind a bad review, timing, tone, and circumstance can make it feel obvious. Trustpilot needs more than a hunch to act on a conflict-of-interest claim, the same way any platform needs evidence rather than an accusation. The evidence-gathering process looks similar to spotting a coordinated fake-review pattern on any platform, even though the policy category differs by platform.

What actually supports a conflict-of-interest claim

  • No matching customer record. The foundation of most Trustpilot disputes still applies here: can you show no transaction matches the reviewer's name, email, or any details they reference.
  • A connection to a competing business. A reviewer's public profile, LinkedIn, or other accounts sometimes directly show employment at or ownership of a competing company.
  • A pattern across multiple reviews from the same source. If you can show several negative reviews tracing back to related accounts or a coordinated timing pattern, that strengthens a single report considerably.
  • Direct evidence of intent. Rare but decisive, a screenshot or message where the connection to a competitor is stated outright.

How to actually file this

Use Trustpilot's standard flagging process through your Business account, and select the conflict-of-interest category specifically rather than a generic complaint. Lay out the connection clearly and factually, what you found and how, rather than framing it as an accusation without support. A report that reads as evidence-based moves differently than one that reads as a grievance.

What happens if you can't prove the connection

If you suspect a competitor but can't substantiate it, the review still gets evaluated on the same "genuine experience" grounds every review does, no matching customer record is still a legitimate basis for a report on its own, even without the competitor angle attached. Don't let an unprovable suspicion stop you from filing on the grounds you can actually support.

Why this matters beyond the one review

A business genuinely facing a sustained pattern of competitor interference is dealing with a bigger problem than any single review, and it's worth knowing that Trustpilot's process differs from Google's in real ways, so expectations stay realistic even in a case with a strong underlying argument.

Should you confront the competitor directly?

It's tempting to reach out to a suspected competitor directly, but this rarely produces anything useful and can escalate a situation that's better handled through the platform's own process. If you do have hard evidence of a conflict of interest, that evidence belongs in your Trustpilot report, not in a direct confrontation that creates no documented record and gives the other side a chance to cover their tracks.

Protecting yourself from the same accusation

It's worth applying the same standard to your own team: reviewing a competitor's listing is fine, posting a review of their business, even an honest one, when you have a clear competitive stake in the outcome is the exact conflict-of-interest problem this policy exists to prevent. Holding your own team to that standard keeps your hands clean if a dispute ever goes the other way.
