What to Do If Someone Creates a Fake Review or Complaint Site About Your Business

GrowGigs Team

A general complaint board hosting posts about thousands of businesses is one problem. A site built specifically about yours, "[Your Business Name] Reviews" or "[Your Business Name] Complaints" as the entire domain, registered by someone with a grudge, is a more targeted and often more serious problem to deal with.

Why a dedicated site is different from a general complaint board

A general complaint board hosts material about many businesses at once and often refuses removal as a matter of blanket policy. A site built around one specific business is usually a different kind of actor entirely, frequently a single aggrieved individual, a former employee, or a competitor, and that difference opens up options a general complaint board doesn't.

Does it use your business name or branding?

If the site uses your actual business name, logo, or a confusingly similar domain in a way that creates brand confusion, that's a trademark issue on top of whatever content is published there. This matters because trademark reporting channels (to the host, registrar, or platform) are often more responsive than general content complaints, since brand misuse creates clearer liability for whoever's hosting it.

Is the content actually false, or just unflattering?

The same honest line applies here as everywhere else: a site presenting false statements of fact as fact can qualify as defamatory content, while a site that's harsh but factually accurate doesn't become removable just because it's damaging and exists solely to broadcast that.

Identify who's actually behind it

A WHOIS lookup on the domain sometimes reveals the registrant directly, though privacy services frequently mask this. Even when the registrant is hidden, identifying the hosting provider gives you a real point of contact for an abuse report, the same lookup process covered in finding who's hosting any infringing content.

When it crosses into harassment

A site that goes beyond criticizing the business into targeting you or named staff personally, publishing private information, or making threats moves into harassment territory, with its own more urgent reporting path separate from a standard content dispute.

What realistically works

  • Trademark or brand-misuse report to the host or registrar, if your name or branding is used in the domain or site itself.
  • A hosting abuse report citing the specific policy violated, defamation, harassment, or impersonation, whichever actually applies.
  • Direct legal consultation for content that's clearly false and causing real, demonstrable business harm, the same escalation point that applies to any severe, unresolved content dispute regardless of the specific site involved.

What to expect realistically

A dedicated single-business complaint site run by one determined individual can be more persistent than a general complaint board, since there's no corporate policy weighing takedown requests against site traffic. Resolution sometimes takes longer and may require sustained pressure across multiple channels rather than a single successful report.

Don't let it dominate how people find you

While pursuing removal, make sure the site isn't the first thing a prospective customer sees when searching your name. Strong, current content about your actual business, your website, your Google Business Profile, recent genuine reviews, gives search results something more relevant and more favorable to show alongside or instead of a single hostile site, regardless of how long formal removal takes.

Document the financial impact if it's significant

If the site is demonstrably costing you business, lost inquiries you can trace directly to it, a measurable dip in bookings after it appeared, keep records of that impact. Beyond supporting a stronger case with hosts or registrars, this kind of documentation is exactly what a legal consultation needs if the situation escalates to that point.
