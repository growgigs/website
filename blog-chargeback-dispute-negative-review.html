What's the Difference Between a Chargeback Dispute and a Negative Review?

GrowGigs Team

A customer who's unhappy with a purchase has two genuinely separate ways to express it, filing a chargeback with their bank or card issuer, or leaving a negative review on a public platform, and businesses that conflate the two tend to respond to the wrong problem with the wrong tool. Understanding which lever a customer actually pulled changes what you can realistically do about it.

A chargeback is a financial dispute, not public speech

A chargeback runs entirely through the banking system, the customer contacts their card issuer, the issuer contacts your payment processor, and the money itself is what's at stake, not your public reputation. Nobody outside that private financial loop sees a chargeback happen, no prospective customer researching your business will ever stumble across one, which makes it a fundamentally different kind of problem than a visible negative review.

A negative review is public reputation, not a financial claim

A review lives on a platform like Google or Trustpilot, visible to anyone researching your business, and doesn't move any money on its own, it's pure reputational signal. A customer can leave a scathing review without ever disputing the charge, and can dispute the charge without ever mentioning it publicly at all, the two actions are entirely independent of each other.

Why the distinction actually matters for your response

  • A chargeback needs evidence for your processor, delivery confirmation, service records, communication logs, the kind of documentation that settles a financial dispute, not a public rebuttal.
  • A review needs a public-facing response or a platform report, covered in what separates reporting a review from disputing it, since the audience here is anyone reading it, not a bank.
  • Winning one doesn't resolve the other. Successfully fighting a chargeback does nothing to a negative review already live on Google, and getting a review removed doesn't un-trigger a chargeback already filed with the card issuer.

The customer who does both is the hardest case

Some genuinely dissatisfied customers pursue both paths at once, filing the chargeback to get their money back and posting the review to warn others, and treating that combination as one single complaint to resolve misses that each needs its own entirely separate process and its own evidence trail. Handle the financial dispute through your processor's actual chargeback procedure, and handle the public review through the platform's own reporting or response tools, in parallel rather than assuming one resolution covers both.

Chargeback abuse is a known, documented problem

Some customers file a chargeback after already receiving the product or service, sometimes called friendly fraud, and separately leave a negative review as supporting "evidence" for their bank dispute, essentially trying to build a paper trail across two unrelated systems. Document your side thoroughly in both places if this pattern looks deliberate, since processors and platforms each weigh their own evidence independently and won't automatically trust a claim just because it appears consistently across both channels.

A review mentioning a chargeback is still just a review

If a customer's public review explicitly mentions that they also disputed the charge, that review is still governed entirely by the platform's own content policies, the same as any other review you might consider reporting, the chargeback reference inside it doesn't change what you can do about the review itself. Evaluate the review on its own merits, truthful or not, policy-violating or not, independent of whatever happened on the financial side.

Build separate playbooks for each

Train whoever handles customer complaints to recognize which lane a given complaint is actually in immediately, financial dispute or public reputation issue, since misrouting one into the other's process wastes time and genuinely resolves nothing. A clear, separate playbook for each keeps your team responding with the right tool instead of improvising a one-size-fits-all reaction to two problems that were never actually the same problem.
