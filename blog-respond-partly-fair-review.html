How to Respond to a Review That's Partly Fair and Partly Wrong

GrowGigs Team

The hardest reviews to deal with aren't the clearly fake ones or the clearly fair ones, they're the ones that are both: a real complaint tangled up with something that didn't actually happen. Here's how to untangle it in a response.

Why this type of review doesn't qualify for removal

Platforms remove reviews for policy violations, not for being partially inaccurate. A review that's mostly a legitimate complaint with some exaggeration or a misremembered detail mixed in is still, on the whole, a genuine review from a genuine customer. Reporting it as fake or inaccurate when the core of it is true is unlikely to succeed, and treating it that way publicly can look worse than just addressing it directly.

Separate the two parts before you write anything

Read the review twice: once for what actually happened, the service issue, the wait time, the product defect, and once for what's stated as fact but isn't accurate, a wrong price, a claim about something that didn't occur, a detail that doesn't match your records. Writing a response without doing this first is how businesses end up either over-apologizing for something that didn't happen or dismissing a complaint that was legitimate.

Acknowledge the real part first

Open by addressing what was actually true, specifically. "You're right that the wait was longer than it should have been that day" lands very differently than a generic "we're sorry you had a bad experience." Specificity signals you read the review and aren't just running a template, both to the original reviewer and to anyone else reading it later.

Correct the inaccurate part, without being defensive

This is the part that trips people up. Correct it factually and briefly, not combatively. "Our records show the order was delivered on the 14th, not the 17th as mentioned" states the correction without accusing the reviewer of lying. Avoid blanket denials like "that's simply not true" or "we never", they read as defensive even when accurate, and they undercut the credibility of the part where you were right to acknowledge the complaint.

Keep it short and move the resolution offline

A long public back-and-forth rarely helps either side. Acknowledge, correct the specific inaccuracy, and offer a way to continue the conversation privately, a phone number, an email, a direct contact. This shows anyone reading that you're willing to resolve it directly, without turning the review thread into an ongoing dispute.

What this accomplishes that removal couldn't

A response doesn't erase the review, but it does something a removal process can't: it gives future readers your side of the inaccurate part while still showing you took the legitimate complaint seriously. Most people reading reviews are evaluating how a business handles problems, not expecting a spotless record. A well-handled mixed review often reads better to a prospective customer than a perfect rating with no visible responses at all.

A quick checklist before you post

Does the response acknowledge something specific and true? Does it correct the inaccurate claim factually, without accusation? Is it short enough that someone would actually read the whole thing? And does it offer a next step rather than ending on the correction? If all four are true, it's ready to post.

What to do if you're not sure which parts are accurate

Sometimes the inaccurate detail isn't obviously wrong, it's a judgment call, a different recollection of a conversation, a dispute over what was promised versus what was delivered. In those cases, don't assert a correction you can't actually back up with records. It's fine to acknowledge the disagreement directly: "We see this differently, but we'd rather sort it out with you directly than go back and forth here" still shows good faith without staking out a factual claim you can't support.

Why rushing this response usually backfires

A review that combines a fair complaint with an inaccuracy often triggers a fast, emotional reply, especially when the inaccurate part feels unfair. That's exactly when the acknowledgment gets skipped and the response reads as purely defensive. Taking even a few minutes to separate the two threads before writing anything produces a response that holds up much better to anyone reading it later, including the original reviewer.
