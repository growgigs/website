NAP Consistency: Why It Matters for Local SEO

GrowGigs Team

NAP stands for Name, Address, and Phone number, the three basic identifiers of your business. It sounds too simple to matter, but inconsistency across the web is one of the more common, quietly damaging local SEO problems.

Why consistency is a signal in itself

Search engines cross-reference your business information across many sources, your website, your Google Business Profile, directories, industry listings, to build confidence that they're looking at the same real business everywhere. When those details don't match, that confidence weakens, which can dilute how strongly all those individual signals contribute to your local ranking.

Common ways NAP data drifts

  • A past business move. The old address lingers on directories you set up years ago and forgot about.
  • Formatting differences. "St." versus "Street," "Suite 200" versus "#200," a phone number with or without a country code, these seem trivial but add up across many listings.
  • A phone number change. Especially common when a business switches providers or adds a dedicated line, and old listings never get updated.
  • Duplicate listings. Multiple Google Business Profile entries or directory entries for the same location, sometimes created accidentally, competing with each other and splitting signals.

Where to check

Start with the sources that matter most: your website's footer and contact page, your Google Business Profile, and the major directories for your industry (Yelp, Facebook, industry-specific listing sites). A simple search of your business name plus your city will also surface listings you may have forgotten existed.

How to fix inconsistencies

Pick one canonical format for your name, address, and phone number, and use that exact format everywhere going forward. Update your website first (since it's the source of truth), then your Google Business Profile, then work through directories one at a time. For duplicate listings, most platforms have a process to merge or remove the outdated one rather than leaving both active.

This isn't a one-time fix

If you move, change your number, or rebrand, the same drift happens again unless you deliberately update every listing, not just your primary ones. A periodic check, once or twice a year, catches drift before it accumulates into a bigger cleanup project.

What this fixes and what it doesn't

Consistent NAP data removes a real obstacle, but it's not a ranking boost on its own, it's closer to removing a source of friction that was working against you. Combined with a complete profile and a healthy review pattern, it's one piece of a foundation rather than a silver bullet.
