How Google Business Profile Q&A Spam Can Hurt Your Listing

GrowGigs Team

Anyone with a Google account can ask a question on your Google Business Profile, and anyone, not just you, can answer it. That openness is exactly what makes the Q&A section an underused vector for damage: a fake question with a misleading self-answer sits on your listing looking like official information.

How Q&A spam actually works

Anyone can post a question, and anyone, including the original asker, can answer it before you ever see it. A bad actor can post a leading question, "Is it true this business overcharges?", then immediately answer it themselves with a fabricated "yes," and both the question and the false answer sit publicly on your profile looking exactly like genuine customer-sourced information.

Why this is more damaging than it sounds

  • It displays prominently on the profile, often above the fold, where a prospective customer sees it before scrolling to reviews.
  • It reads as information, not opinion, giving a fabricated claim more apparent credibility than a one-star review carries, since it's framed as a factual answer rather than a personal complaint.
  • You're not notified reliably, unlike reviews, many businesses don't actively monitor the Q&A section and can go weeks without noticing a damaging entry.

What actually qualifies for removal

Google's policies prohibit spam, off-topic content, and content violating other guidelines, false factual claims about pricing, policies, or safety fall under this. A question-and-self-answer pair that's clearly fabricated to create a false impression, rather than a genuine customer inquiry, is reportable using the same flagging mechanism available on any Q&A entry.

Monitor it the way you'd monitor reviews

Treat the Q&A section as part of your regular profile monitoring routine, not an afterthought, the same discipline covered in keeping a Google Business Profile actively maintained. A quick weekly check catches a spam entry before it's sat visible for a month.

Answer legitimate questions yourself, proactively

The best defense against a bad-faith answer winning visibility is posting the correct answer yourself first, as the business, for any question that comes in. Google often prioritizes a business-provided answer in display order, and a legitimate, prompt answer from you crowds out the opportunity for someone else to answer first with something false.

Seed your own FAQ-style questions

Posting your own genuinely useful questions and answers, your hours, parking, accepted payment methods, fills the space with accurate information and makes an eventual spam entry stand out as obviously inconsistent by comparison, rather than blending into a mostly-empty section.

This is a smaller piece of a larger profile-integrity picture

Q&A spam sits alongside other profile vulnerabilities worth watching, like unauthorized "Suggest an Edit" changes to your listing details. Both exploit the same underlying openness that makes Google Business Profile collaborative by design, and both require the same basic habit: checking your own listing regularly rather than assuming it stays as you left it.

When a competitor is clearly behind it

Repeated, pointed, or suspiciously well-informed fake questions targeting specific operational details can suggest a competitor rather than a random troll. The response is the same either way, report the content through Google's flagging tool and post the correct answer yourself, but recognizing the pattern helps you decide whether it's worth the extra effort of monitoring more closely going forward rather than treating it as a one-off.

Assign someone the actual responsibility

A profile nobody specifically owns is a profile nobody actually checks, Q&A spam included. Whether that's an owner, a manager, or whoever already handles review responses, name one person responsible for a regular glance at the full profile, reviews and Q&A together, rather than assuming it falls under someone's job by default when it falls under no one's in practice.
