Can a Yelp Review Be Removed for Violating Yelp's Self-Promotion Policy?

GrowGigs Team

A one-star review on your Yelp page spends most of its text praising a different business, sometimes a direct competitor, sometimes the reviewer's own company, rather than actually describing an experience with you. Yelp treats this as its own distinct policy violation, separate from a standard complaint about quality or service, and it has a real removal path if you can show what's actually going on.

What Yelp's self-promotion policy actually covers

Yelp's content guidelines prohibit using a review to advertise, promote, or direct attention toward a different business, whether that's the reviewer's own company, a client's business, or simply a competitor they're steering readers toward. The review itself doesn't need to be entirely promotional, even a review that includes one clear promotional plug alongside otherwise relevant content can qualify, since the policy targets the promotional intent rather than requiring the whole post to be an ad.

Recognizing the pattern before you report it

  • Direct mentions of a competing business by name, especially phrased as "go to [competitor] instead," a clear signal the review exists to redirect business elsewhere.
  • A reviewer whose own profile or activity reveals a business affiliation, a competitor's owner or employee posting under a personal account rather than disclosing the connection.
  • Content that reads like an ad more than an experience, listing a competitor's prices, hours, or services in detail rather than describing anything that actually happened with your business.

Why this is a distinct category from a standard fake review

A self-promotion violation doesn't necessarily mean the reviewer never visited your business at all, the review could describe a genuine, if harshly critical, experience while still crossing the line by explicitly pitching a competitor in the same post. That combination matters because Yelp evaluates the promotional content on its own policy grounds, separate from whatever genuine or disputed experience the rest of the review describes, which is different from a review from someone who never actually visited at all.

How to document a self-promotion violation

Screenshot the review in full before reporting it, since the content can be edited or deleted after the fact in ways that complicate your case later. Highlight the specific promotional language in your report rather than simply flagging the review as generally unfair, Yelp's moderators respond to a precisely identified policy violation far better than a broad complaint about tone or fairness.

What Yelp typically needs to act on this

Yelp's moderation team looks for content that clearly promotes a different business rather than relays an actual customer experience with yours, so your report should point directly at the promotional language itself, quoted if possible, rather than relying on your own characterization of what the review is doing. A report that simply says "this seems like an ad" without identifying specific promotional text is much easier for a moderator to dismiss than one that quotes the exact line doing the promoting.

Competitor self-promotion sometimes overlaps with fake reviews entirely

Some of the most blatant cases combine both issues, a competitor's staff member posting a one-star review that never visited your business and explicitly plugs their own company in the same breath, which gives you two separate, independently reportable violations in a single post. Reporting both angles, the fabricated experience and the promotional content, tends to strengthen a case that might look borderline on either ground alone.

Don't assume every mention of a competitor is promotional

A reviewer comparing your service unfavorably to a competitor they've also used, without any promotional push or direct endorsement, is ordinary comparative criticism rather than a policy violation, and reporting it as self-promotion when it isn't tends to waste your credibility with the platform. Reserve this report for reviews that are actually functioning as an advertisement, not simply ones that happen to mention another business by name.
