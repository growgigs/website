Do Negative Reviews Ever Expire or Age Out on Google?

GrowGigs Team

It's a reasonable assumption, old content fades online all the time, but it doesn't apply to Google reviews the way people expect. Here's what actually happens to a review over time.

The short answer

No. A review stays on your Google Business Profile indefinitely unless it's removed, either by the reviewer deleting it themselves or by Google taking it down for a policy violation. There's no automatic expiration, no "ages out after a year," nothing built into the system that quietly retires old reviews.

Why this misconception is so common

Other things about your profile do change with time: your overall star rating shifts as new reviews come in, and the most recent reviews are what show up first by default. That visual effect, old reviews sliding further down the list as new ones accumulate, can look like fading or expiring. The review itself hasn't gone anywhere, it's just less prominent in a list that defaults to newest-first. It's an easy mix-up, the rating number moves, the list reorders, and it's natural to assume the underlying content is changing too when really only its position is.

What actually changes a review's visibility

Sort order is the biggest factor. Someone has to actively change the sort to "most relevant" or scroll past recent reviews to see an old one, by default they're seeing what's newest. Review volume also matters a great deal: a single bad review from two years ago carries far more visible weight on a profile with a dozen total reviews than on one with several hundred.

What does actually remove a review

Three things, realistically: the reviewer deletes their own review, the reviewer's Google account gets removed or banned, which can take their reviews with it, though this isn't guaranteed or quick, or Google removes it after a successful policy-violation report. None of these happen just because time has passed.

So what should you actually do about an old bad review

If it's still violating a real policy, fake, not based on a genuine experience, abusive, it's eligible for removal the same way a brand-new review would be. Age doesn't disqualify it or make it harder to report. If it's a genuine, if unflattering, review, the more effective move is building up recent positive reviews, which pushes it down the default sort and dilutes its weight on your overall rating, rather than waiting for something that isn't going to happen on its own.

A related question: what about reviews from deleted or inactive accounts

If a reviewer deletes their own Google account, their reviews can disappear along with it, but this isn't something you can trigger or predict, and it isn't guaranteed to happen at all. An inactive account (one the owner simply stopped using) behaves no differently from an active one as far as the review's visibility goes, the review stays exactly as it was. This isn't a path worth waiting on any more than hoping a review will time out on its own.

Does responding to a review change any of this

No. Replying to a review doesn't reset its date, change its position in the default sort, or affect whether it's eligible for removal later. A response is worth doing for what it communicates to other people reading the profile, not because it alters anything about how Google handles the review itself.

The honest takeaway

Nothing about Google's system causes reviews to fade with age. If an old review bothers you, your options are the same ones available for a brand-new one: report it if it violates policy, or outweigh it with more recent positive reviews if it doesn't.
