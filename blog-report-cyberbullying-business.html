How to Report Cyberbullying Content About Your Business

GrowGigs Team

Targeted harassment aimed at your business or your staff is different from an ordinary negative review, and most platforms treat it as a distinct, more serious category. Here's how to actually get it addressed.

What counts as cyberbullying versus a harsh review

A negative review criticizes an experience. Cyberbullying targets people, repeated harassment of your staff by name, threats, degrading personal attacks, or coordinated pile-ons meant to intimidate rather than inform. If the content is aimed at humiliating or frightening someone rather than describing a business interaction, it's likely crossed into harassment territory that most platforms' policies cover separately from review content.

Document before you report

Screenshot everything: the content, the account posting it, timestamps, and any pattern (multiple posts, multiple accounts that appear coordinated). Harassment content sometimes gets deleted by the poster once they realize they're being reported, so capture evidence first.

Platform reporting tools

  • Social platforms (Facebook, Instagram, X, TikTok). Each has a harassment or bullying-specific report category, separate from general content complaints, use it rather than a generic report.
  • Google. Reviews containing harassment or threats can be flagged through your Business Profile under content policy violations.
  • Forums and comment sections. Look for the site's own reporting tool first; if there isn't one, contact the site's host directly (identifiable via a WHOIS lookup) and cite their terms of service.

When to escalate beyond the platform

If harassment includes direct threats of violence, doxxing (publishing private information like home addresses), or a pattern that doesn't stop after reporting, this moves beyond a standard content removal request. Threats of violence can be reported to law enforcement, and a documented pattern of harassment may support a formal legal request depending on your jurisdiction, this is worth discussing with an attorney if it escalates.

Protecting staff who are personally targeted

If an employee is being personally harassed (not just your business), encourage them to report the content on their own account as well, since platforms sometimes weigh a direct target's report differently than a business report about content aimed at someone else. Keep them informed and involved rather than handling it entirely on their behalf without their knowledge.

What to expect

Platforms generally treat harassment and threats as priority content, faster action than a standard dispute, but "faster" still isn't instant. A clear, well-documented report citing the specific policy violated moves faster than a general complaint that something is "bullying."
