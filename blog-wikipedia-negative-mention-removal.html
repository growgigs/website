Can You Get a Negative Mention Removed From a Wikipedia Article About Your Business?

GrowGigs Team

Wikipedia articles rank well, get cited by other sites, and read as neutral and authoritative to anyone researching your business, which is exactly why a negative line buried in one carries more weight than the same claim almost anywhere else. The process for getting it changed is nothing like reporting a review, and treating it the same way usually makes things worse.

Why Wikipedia isn't like other platforms

There's no removal request form, no business-owner portal, and no customer support line. Wikipedia is edited by volunteers under a published set of policies, and anyone, including you, can technically edit an article. The catch is Wikipedia's conflict-of-interest guideline strongly discourages subjects of an article from editing it directly, especially to remove unflattering but sourced content, and doing so is likely to get flagged, reverted, and can draw more scrutiny to the article than if you'd left it alone.

What actually qualifies for removal or correction

  • Unsourced or poorly sourced claims. Wikipedia requires verifiable citations to reliable, independent sources. A negative statement with no citation, or one sourced to a blog, forum post, or press release rather than independent reporting, is a legitimate target.
  • Factual inaccuracies. A wrong date, a misattributed quote, a settled lawsuit described as ongoing, these are correctable through evidence, not opinion.
  • Content that misrepresents its own source. If the cited article doesn't actually say what the Wikipedia text claims it says, that's a verifiable discrepancy worth raising.
  • Outdated information no longer reflected in current, reliable coverage. This is narrower than it sounds, "it's old" alone isn't grounds for removal if it's accurate and sourced; it has to be superseded by better, newer sourcing.

What generally doesn't qualify: accurate, well-sourced coverage you simply don't like. Wikipedia's policies don't treat "this hurts our reputation" as a reason to remove accurate content, which is a meaningfully different bar than a platform review report.

The right way to request a change

Every Wikipedia article has an associated "Talk" page, use it to lay out exactly what's wrong and why, citing the specific policy (verifiability, neutrality, or sourcing) that applies, rather than arguing the content is simply unfair. If you have a direct conflict of interest, disclose it on the talk page and propose the edit rather than making it yourself, volunteer editors are far more receptive to a transparent, policy-grounded request than to an anonymous edit that looks like it's covering something up.

When there's no article-level fix

If the underlying claim is accurate and well-sourced, Wikipedia's own process won't remove it, full stop, and no legitimate service can guarantee otherwise despite what some reputation firms imply. In that situation, the realistic move is addressing the underlying coverage at its source (see removing old news articles for that separate process) or focusing on building current, accurate information elsewhere that gives a fuller picture when someone researches you.

What if the whole article is the problem

Occasionally the issue isn't one negative line but the article's existence at all, for a small or local business that doesn't clearly meet Wikipedia's notability standard (independent, substantial coverage in multiple reliable sources). In that narrow case, there's a separate process: proposing deletion through the site's deletion-discussion pages, where other editors weigh in on whether the subject actually meets the notability bar. This only applies when notability is genuinely in question, not as a workaround for "I'd rather this article not exist." A clearly notable business with one unflattering but sourced paragraph won't succeed on a notability argument, that's a sourcing or neutrality fix, not a deletion case.

A note on paid editing

Wikipedia requires paid editors to disclose that relationship on their user page and the relevant talk pages, undisclosed paid editing violates the platform's terms and tends to get discovered and reverted, often with the business's name now associated with the violation itself. Any legitimate help here works through disclosure and talk-page proposals, not quiet direct edits.
