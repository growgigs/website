What's the Difference Between Review Gating and Legitimate Review Solicitation?

GrowGigs Team

Asking satisfied customers to leave a review is normal, encouraged, and something most review platforms are fine with. Asking customers how their experience went first, then only directing the happy ones to post publicly while routing complaints somewhere private, is a different practice entirely, one with a name, a regulatory history, and consequences for businesses that get caught doing it.

What review gating actually is

Review gating is the practice of pre-screening customers, typically through a private survey or star-rating prompt, before deciding who gets invited to leave a public review. A customer who indicates a positive experience gets a direct link to Google or another review platform; a customer who indicates a negative one gets redirected to a private feedback form instead, never reaching the public review step at all. The result is a review profile that's been selectively curated rather than reflecting the full range of actual customer experiences.

Why this is treated as a regulated practice, not just bad etiquette

Regulators, the FTC among them, have specifically targeted review gating as a form of deceptive advertising, because a selectively filtered set of public reviews misrepresents overall customer sentiment to anyone relying on those reviews to make a decision. This distinguishes it from simply having a good reputation that happens to be reflected in reviews, the violation is in the deliberate filtering mechanism itself, not in the fact that most reviews turn out positive.

What legitimate review solicitation looks like instead

  • Ask every customer the same way. Send the same review request or link to all customers who completed a transaction, regardless of any indication of how their experience went.
  • Don't pre-filter by sentiment. If you want to understand satisfaction internally, a separate, non-gating survey that doesn't determine who gets a public review invitation is the legitimate version of that goal.
  • Make the ask simple and consistent. A templated request sent at a predictable point (after service completion, after delivery) without routing based on predicted sentiment is standard, acceptable solicitation.

Where this connects to NPS and other internal surveys

An NPS survey that stays entirely internal, used only to track satisfaction trends, is not review gating on its own, the violation specifically requires using that sentiment data to determine who gets directed toward a public review and who doesn't. A business can run internal satisfaction surveys and ask for public reviews separately and consistently, as long as the survey results aren't the gate deciding who's invited to post.

What a reasonable, compliant review request process looks like

A single, uniform review request sent to all customers at a consistent point in their experience, with no pre-screening step determining who receives it, satisfies both the platforms' own terms and the regulatory standard. If some customers naturally decline to leave a review while others do, that's normal response-rate variation, not gating, the line is about whether your process decides in advance who gets asked based on how happy they seem.

A common gray area worth clarifying

Offering every customer a choice of where to leave feedback, a public review platform or a direct line to your team, isn't gating as long as the choice itself isn't steered by a sentiment prediction and every customer sees the same options regardless of how the interaction went. The violation specifically requires an upstream filter deciding who sees which path; a neutral menu of equally available options presented to everyone doesn't have that filtering mechanism, even though it superficially resembles a gating flow.

What happens if a past campaign turns out to have been gating

If you realize a review-solicitation tool or process you've been using actually routes by sentiment, the fix is switching to a uniform process going forward, not retroactively trying to remove existing reviews that came through the gated flow, since removing reviews solely because of how they were originally solicited isn't a standard removal ground on any major platform. Going forward with a compliant, consistent ask is the practical remedy; the reviews already collected simply stay as part of your profile.
