Do Reviews From Other Locations Count Toward My Listing?

GrowGigs Team

Multi-location businesses often assume reviews work like a brand-wide reputation score. They don't, each Google Business Profile listing has its own separate reviews and rating. Here's what that actually means in practice.

The short answer

No. Each physical location with its own Google Business Profile listing has its own set of reviews and its own star rating, visible to anyone searching or viewing that specific listing. A five-star rating at your downtown location doesn't show up on, or factor into, the rating at your location across town. They're entirely separate from a public-facing standpoint.

Why this surprises people

It's a reasonable assumption since your brand is the same everywhere, the business name, the services, the overall reputation feel unified from the inside. But Google treats each physical address as its own entity in local search, because that's what's actually relevant to someone searching nearby, they care about the specific location they'd visit, not an abstract brand-wide average.

What can look like pooling, but isn't

A few things create the appearance of shared reviews without actually being that: a customer leaves a review but accidentally selects the wrong nearby location from a dropdown or autocomplete, misattributing it, two locations close together sometimes get confused by customers checking in or reviewing from memory rather than checking the exact listing, and some business management dashboards show an aggregate view across all your locations for your own internal tracking, which is a very different thing from what's publicly visible on each listing's search result.

What to do about a review attached to the wrong location

If you believe a review was left for the wrong location, mistaken identity, a nearby competitor with a similar name, an actual address mix-up, that's worth investigating and, if confirmed, reporting through Google's standard process. This isn't the same as a policy-violation removal case, it's a factual correction, but it follows a similar reporting path.

The practical implication for building your reputation

Since reviews don't pool, you can't rely on a strong rating at one location to carry a newer or weaker one. Each location needs its own, ongoing effort to generate genuine reviews, a location that just opened genuinely starts near zero, regardless of how established the brand is elsewhere. This is worth planning for explicitly in a multi-location rollout rather than assuming reputation transfers automatically.

What does carry over across locations

Brand-level things do matter even though reviews don't pool: your overall reputation among people who've visited multiple locations, your name recognition, and how consistent your information is across listings (the NAP consistency covered elsewhere on this blog) all contribute to how customers perceive the brand as a whole. It's just that none of it shows up as a shared number on an individual listing's public page, it's a qualitative brand effect, not a quantitative one that Google surfaces per location.

A related question worth answering directly

If one location gets a wave of fake or policy-violating reviews, does reporting that pattern affect other locations too? No, each listing's removal process is independent. A flag filed against reviews on one listing doesn't apply to, or draw scrutiny to, your other locations' listings. Each needs to be evaluated and reported on its own, which is more work across a multi-location footprint but keeps each location's case clean and specific.

The bottom line

Treat each location's Google Business Profile as its own standalone reputation, because that's functionally what it is to anyone searching locally. Brand consistency matters for how customers perceive you overall, but the actual review count and star rating that show up in search are specific to each physical listing, with nothing carrying over from the others.
