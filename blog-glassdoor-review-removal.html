Glassdoor Review Removal: What's Different From Google and Trustpilot

GrowGigs Team

Glassdoor gets lumped in with Google and Trustpilot in a lot of reputation-management conversations, but it's a fundamentally different kind of platform, and that difference changes how removal actually works.

The core difference: reviewers are employees, not customers

Google and Trustpilot reviews come from people who bought something or used a service. Glassdoor reviews come from current or former employees, and in some cases, interview candidates, writing about what it's actually like to work somewhere: management, culture, compensation, work-life balance. A bad Glassdoor review isn't about your product, it's about your workplace, which means the grounds for disputing one are different too.

Glassdoor reviews are anonymous by default

This is the biggest practical difference. On Google and Trustpilot, you can often identify or at least investigate whether a specific reviewer matches your customer records. Glassdoor protects reviewer anonymity specifically to shield employees and former employees from retaliation, and that anonymity generally holds even from the employer. This makes "prove this person was never actually an employee" a much harder case to build than the equivalent claim on a customer review platform.

What actually qualifies for removal

Glassdoor's content guidelines cover things like harassment or discriminatory language, disclosure of confidential or proprietary company information, personal attacks on a named individual, and content not based on a genuine employment experience. A negative but genuine review about management style, pay, or culture, even a harshly worded one, generally doesn't violate these guidelines just for being critical. The same principle that applies on Google and Trustpilot holds here: being unflattering isn't a policy violation on its own.

How to actually flag a review

Businesses manage this through Glassdoor's Employer Center, the same dashboard used to manage your company profile and respond to reviews. Flagged content gets evaluated against Glassdoor's specific guidelines, not against whether the business disputes the opinion expressed.

Why "this wasn't a real employee" is harder to prove here

On a customer review platform, a records check, no matching order, no matching account, can support a fake-review claim. On Glassdoor, there's no equivalent "customer record" to check against for most businesses, especially larger ones with high turnover, and anonymity means you often can't verify identity even informally. This isn't a dead end, reviews referencing specific, checkable facts, a role that never existed, a location you don't operate, a timeframe before the company existed, can still support a report, but the evidence path looks different from what works on Google or Trustpilot.

What tends to get overlooked

Businesses sometimes try to flag a Glassdoor review the same way they'd flag a Google review, treating "this is unfair" or "this doesn't reflect our culture" as grounds on its own. Neither works on any of these platforms, but it's a more common mistake on Glassdoor specifically, because the content is about something as subjective as workplace culture, where "unfair" and "inaccurate" can feel interchangeable even though only one of them maps to an actual guideline violation.

Interview reviews are a separate category worth knowing about

Beyond employee reviews, Glassdoor also hosts interview experience reviews from candidates who may never have been hired. These follow a similar structure, genuine experiences stay up, guideline violations don't, but it's worth knowing this is a distinct content type if you're auditing what's actually been posted about your company, not just checking the employee review section.

The bottom line

Glassdoor's process shares the same basic shape as other review platforms, flag it, cite a specific violation, provide what evidence you can, but the anonymity model and the fact that reviewers are employees rather than customers mean the practical path to a successful report looks noticeably different.
