Does Verifying Your Google Business Profile by Video Call Work Differently Than by Postcard?

GrowGigs Team

You create a new Google Business Profile and Google asks to verify it, but the specific method offered isn't always the postcard-in-the-mail process most business owners actually expect going in. Sometimes it's a video call instead, and the difference isn't random, it reflects what kind of business Google thinks it's dealing with.

Google picks the method, not you, in most cases

The verification options offered depend heavily on your business category, location, and account signals, not a preference you actually get to select freely yourself. A standard retail storefront in a well-established category is more likely to get the postcard option; a service-area business, a newer account, or a category Google treats as higher-risk for spam is more likely to be routed toward video verification instead.

What postcard verification actually involves

  • A physical mailer sent to your listed business address, containing a verification code.
  • A real wait, typically one to two weeks for delivery, longer if the address has any delivery quirks.
  • A single code entry once received, after which the profile goes live without further steps.

What video call verification actually involves

  • A scheduled live call with a Google representative, where you show real-time evidence the business is genuinely operating.
  • Specific proof requested live, exterior signage, interior premises, business documentation, sometimes your Business Profile Manager dashboard itself.
  • Faster resolution if it goes well, often resolved within the call itself rather than waiting on mail delivery.

Why a service-area business often gets routed to video

A business with no public storefront, the scenario covered in handling a service-area listing without a visible location, naturally can't rely on postcard delivery to a storefront a customer would ever see, making video verification the more practical option Google offers more often in these cases.

Prepare differently depending on which you get

For postcard verification, simply make sure your mail is actually being monitored at the listed address during the verification window, a missed or lost postcard just means requesting a new one, not a lost cause by any means. For video verification, prepare well in advance: have signage, interior shots, and any licensing or registration documents ready to show live, and do a quick technical check of your camera and connection before the scheduled call even starts.

A failed verification isn't permanent, either way

If postcard delivery fails or the code doesn't work, you can request a new one or sometimes switch methods entirely. If a video call doesn't go well, typically because the evidence presented didn't clearly establish the business, you can usually request another attempt rather than being permanently blocked, though addressing whatever specifically fell short the first time around matters quite a bit before trying again.

Why this connects to broader profile integrity

Google's verification process exists largely to prevent the same kind of listing abuse covered in why profiles get suspended in the first place, fake businesses, duplicate claims, and spam listings. Taking whichever verification method you're offered seriously, and being ready to substantiate your business's legitimacy either way, keeps the process moving rather than creating an avoidable delay before you can start optimizing the profile itself.

What to do if you've waited well beyond the normal window

If a postcard genuinely never arrives after a reasonable wait, or a scheduled video call never materializes, don't just keep waiting indefinitely, request a new verification attempt through Business Profile Manager directly rather than assuming the process will eventually sort itself out. Verification delays compound: every week your profile sits unverified is a week it isn't showing up properly in local search for customers actually looking for you.
