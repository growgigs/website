Does Trustpilot's TrustScore Algorithm Favor Recent Reviews?

GrowGigs Team

A business with a rough patch three years ago and a strong, consistent run since wants to know whether that history still drags the number down today, or whether TrustScore has already moved on. The honest answer sits between a simple average and a fully recency-weighted score.

TrustScore isn't a simple average

Trustpilot has stated publicly that TrustScore incorporates recency, relevance, and reviewer activity rather than functioning as a flat average of every star rating ever posted since the account's founding. This means two businesses with identical total review counts and identical average ratings can still display meaningfully different TrustScores if their review timing and overall distribution differ.

What this means practically

  • Recent reviews carry more practical weight in how the current score reflects your business, a strong run of reviews in the past year moves the needle more than reviews from several years back.
  • Old reviews don't vanish from the number entirely, they simply contribute less to the current calculation than they would in a true average.
  • Review velocity matters alongside rating, a steady, ongoing stream of genuine reviews signals an actively evaluated business differently than a static review count that stopped growing.

This isn't the same as review removal

Understanding TrustScore's weighting doesn't substitute for actually getting a genuinely problematic review removed when it qualifies, the mechanics covered in Trustpilot review removal generally. A bad review still displays publicly and still factors into the score's calculation regardless of how it's weighted, recency weighting softens old damage over time, it doesn't erase a current violation.

Don't try to game the recency weighting

Soliciting a burst of reviews specifically to dilute recent negative ones, rather than through genuine, ongoing customer outreach, risks tripping Trustpilot's own detection systems for unnatural review patterns, the same systems covered in how platforms detect fake review activity generally, even though the specific platform differs. A sudden unnatural spike is itself a red flag, regardless of whether the individual reviews are genuine.

What actually works instead

Consistent, ongoing collection of genuine reviews from real customers, spread naturally over time, is what benefits from recency weighting the way it's actually designed to work. A business actively asking satisfied customers to review at the natural point in their experience, rather than in artificial bursts, builds exactly the steady recent-review pattern that keeps the score reflecting current performance.

Verified business accounts and score legitimacy

A verified paying Trustpilot Business account doesn't change the underlying TrustScore calculation itself, but it does give you access to better tools, direct review invitations, response capabilities, to actually influence the ongoing flow of recent reviews that the algorithm weights more heavily in the first place.

The honest takeaway

A past rough patch matters less over time as genuine, consistent reviews accumulate, but "less" isn't "not at all," and there's no shortcut that substitutes for simply continuing to deliver, and collect feedback on, good service over a sustained period.

Set realistic expectations with your own team

If you're explaining this to staff or a partner anxious about a historical rough patch still hanging over the business, the honest framing is patience plus consistency, not a quick fix. A sudden campaign to flood the profile with reviews reads as exactly the kind of manipulation Trustpilot's systems are built to catch, while a steady, unremarkable stream of real customer feedback is what actually moves the number over the months that follow.

How long does meaningful movement actually take

There's no official timeline Trustpilot publishes for exactly how much weight shifts away from older reviews, but businesses that maintain a consistent review volume typically see their displayed score start reflecting the improved pattern within a few months rather than years. The exact pace depends heavily on your existing review volume, a business with thousands of historical reviews dilutes a rough patch faster in relative terms than one with only a handful.
