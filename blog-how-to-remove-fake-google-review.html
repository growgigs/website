How to Remove a Fake Google Review (Step-by-Step Guide)

GrowGigs Team

A fake review, from a bot, a competitor, or someone who was never actually a customer, is one of the more straightforward removal cases. Here's the exact process for reporting one yourself, and how to tell when it needs more than a standard report.

What actually counts as a "fake" review

Google's policy defines fake reviews narrowly, and it's worth knowing the actual categories before you file a report, because a vague "this isn't fair" report gets ignored:

  • The reviewer was never a customer. They never bought from you, visited your location, or interacted with your business.
  • It's posted by a bot or fake account. Mass-produced reviews, often with generic text reused across many businesses.
  • It's from a competitor. A conflict-of-interest review posted by someone who runs or works for a competing business.
  • It's spam or off-topic. Content unrelated to an actual experience with your business, including ads or copy-pasted text.

Step 1: Screenshot everything first

Reviews and reviewer profiles can be edited or deleted by the person who posted them before you've finished your report. Screenshot the review text, the star rating, the date, and the reviewer's profile (their review history often shows the pattern, like dozens of identical reviews across unrelated businesses).

Step 2: Report it through your Google Business Profile

From your Google Business Profile, find the review under the Reviews tab, click the three-dot menu, and select "Flag as inappropriate." You'll be asked to choose a reason, pick the one that actually matches (fake engagement, spam, conflict of interest, and so on) rather than the closest-sounding option.

Step 3: Wait, then follow up if nothing happens

Google's automated system resolves obvious cases within a few days. If it doesn't act, or rejects the report without explanation, you can escalate through Google's Business Profile support (chat or a support ticket) and reference the specific policy the review violates.

When a first report isn't enough

Google's automated system catches the obvious cases. It's much less reliable when the account has some post history, when the reviewer's connection to your business isn't obvious from the review alone, or when you're dealing with several reviews at once across locations. That's usually where a documented, single well-evidenced report gets further than repeated identical flags.

What doesn't work, and can backfire

Reporting the same review from many different accounts to manufacture volume isn't a shortcut, it's coordinated inauthentic reporting, which violates Google's own policies and risks your Business Profile getting flagged. One well-documented report beats fifty low-effort ones.
