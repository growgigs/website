What to Do If Your Google Business Profile's Website Link Is Broken or Outdated

GrowGigs Team

A customer taps the website button on your Google Business Profile and lands on a 404 page, an expired domain, or a site you redesigned two years ago and quietly forgot still has old links pointing at it elsewhere. It's an easy problem to fix and an easy one to overlook entirely until a customer mentions it.

Where this actually comes from

  • A domain change or migration where the profile's website field never got updated to match.
  • A site redesign that restructured URLs, leaving the specific page your profile links to pointing at a path that no longer exists.
  • A lapsed domain, more common than businesses expect when a domain renewal gets missed during a busy season or an ownership transition.
  • A third-party platform URL, a social media page or a marketplace listing used as the "website," which can change structure or get removed without your business controlling it.

Why this costs more than it seems to

A broken website link doesn't just fail silently, it actively damages trust at the exact moment a customer was ready to learn more or take action. Someone who clicks through to a dead page is less likely to try calling or visiting instead, they've already formed an impression that the business isn't paying attention, and that impression travels further than the single lost click.

Fix it directly, and quickly

Update the website URL field in Business Profile Manager as soon as you notice the problem, this is a direct self-service edit with no approval delay, unlike disputing a closure status or hours error. There's no reason this particular fix should sit unresolved for more than a few minutes once you're aware of it.

Check it regularly, not just when a customer complains

Click your own website link from the live, public-facing profile periodically, not just from memory or your own bookmarked version of the site. This is a small addition to the same ongoing profile monitoring habit covered in checking for Q&A spam, both are easy to let slide precisely because nothing alerts you automatically when they break.

Point to the most useful page, not just the homepage

If your business has location-specific or service-specific landing pages, consider whether the homepage is really the most useful destination, or whether a more targeted page converts better for someone clicking through directly from a map search. This isn't about fixing a break, it's about making sure a working link is actually doing its job well, not just technically functioning.

Watch for this after any site migration specifically

Whenever you redesign or migrate your website, add "update every third-party profile link" to the launch checklist, Google Business Profile, directory listings, social bios, all of it. This is the single most common moment a broken link problem gets created, and it's entirely preventable with one extra checklist item at exactly the right time.

A broken link compounds with other stale signals

A dead website link rarely shows up alone, it's often one symptom of a profile that's gone generally unattended, alongside other optimization basics slipping at the same time. Treat finding one as a prompt to audit the whole profile, not just patch the single broken field and move on without checking anything else nearby.

Assign someone the actual job of catching this

Like most of these small listing-maintenance issues, a broken link only gets caught quickly when someone is specifically responsible for noticing it, not left to the chance that a customer happens to mention it. Build a quick quarterly click-through check into whoever already manages your profile's reviews or posts, rather than treating it as a separate task nobody actually owns.
