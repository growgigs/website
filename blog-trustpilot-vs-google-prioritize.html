Trustpilot vs. Google Reviews: Which Should You Prioritize Removing First?

GrowGigs Team

Two damaging reviews, one on Google, one on Trustpilot, both eligible for removal, both demanding time and attention. A business with limited bandwidth to pursue both at once needs a real answer for where to start, not just "both matter."

Start with where the traffic actually is

For most businesses, Google reviews sit directly inside the search result and map pack a prospective customer sees first, while Trustpilot requires an extra click to a separate site most searchers never make unless they're already deep in research mode. If a damaging review is costing inbound inquiries, the Google review is usually intercepting more of that traffic simply by sitting at the point of first contact.

But Trustpilot can carry disproportionate weight in certain industries

E-commerce, SaaS, and certain financial or travel verticals often treat a Trustpilot score as a trust signal nearly as important as Google's, sometimes displayed directly on the business's own site via a widget. If that's the case for your business, a damaging Trustpilot review sitting prominently on your own homepage can matter more than its search visibility alone would suggest.

Weigh eligibility strength, not just platform

  • A clear-cut case beats a borderline one on either platform. Don't deprioritize a strong, well-evidenced Trustpilot case just because Google gets more search traffic, if the Google case is weaker and less likely to succeed.
  • Non-customer and off-topic violations tend to be the clearest wins on both platforms, and a clean violation is worth pursuing first regardless of where it sits.
  • A conflict-of-interest case, like a competitor-posted Trustpilot review, can be unusually strong once documented, strong enough to outweigh a weaker Google case even with lower search visibility.

Consider review volume and dilution

A single bad review buried under 200 good ones on a platform with heavy review volume dilutes faster than the same review sitting among a thinner base. If one platform has significantly fewer total reviews, a bad one there has outsized visible impact and may deserve priority purely on that basis. Pull your actual review counts on both platforms before deciding, rather than guessing which one feels more exposed.

Don't let process differences dictate priority by default

Google's reporting process is generally faster to initiate than Trustpilot's own flagging process, but ease of filing shouldn't be the deciding factor on its own. A slower process worth pursuing because the case is strong and the platform matters to your business is still worth starting even if it isn't the quickest win available.

A practical sequencing approach

File the strongest, clearest-violation case first regardless of platform, since momentum and an early win matter more operationally than optimizing for theoretical traffic impact. Then move to the platform carrying more weight for your specific business model and customer base, rather than defaulting to Google simply because it's the more familiar process.

When you genuinely can't choose

If both cases are similarly strong and similarly important to your business, there's no real reason not to pursue both simultaneously rather than sequentially, the two processes don't compete with each other operationally, and running them in parallel costs little beyond the coordination of tracking two cases instead of one.

Don't let one platform's resolution distract from the other

A common mistake once one case starts moving is to quietly deprioritize the second case while celebrating progress on the first one. Track both cases on the same timeline from the start, with the same follow-up discipline, rather than letting whichever platform responds faster absorb all the attention while the other quietly stalls from neglect rather than any real obstacle in the process itself.
