Why Does My Competitor Have Worse Reviews But Still Outrank Me?

GrowGigs Team

You've got the better rating. More recent reviews. Fewer complaints. And yet there they are, sitting above you in the map pack every time you check. It's frustrating, and it's also a sign that reviews aren't the whole story in local ranking, even though they feel like they should be the one that matters most.

Reviews are one input, not the whole algorithm

Google's local ranking system weighs relevance, distance, and prominence together, and review signals are just one component of prominence, alongside things like citation volume, website authority, and overall engagement with the listing. A business can be meaningfully weaker on reviews and still win if it's stronger on the other factors, especially proximity, which carries real weight for a lot of local searches.

Proximity might simply be beating you

If your competitor's listed address is closer to the searcher (or to the center point Google uses for that search), that alone can outweigh a review-quality gap for many queries. This is genuinely one of the most common and least satisfying answers, there's not always a content or optimization fix for being farther from where people are searching from.

Category and profile completeness

A profile with the exact right category, fully filled-out attributes, services listed, and regular posting activity sends stronger relevance and prominence signals than a thinner profile, even one with a better star rating. See how to optimize your Google Business Profile for the full checklist, a surprising number of ranking gaps trace back to basic completeness rather than anything competitive.

Website and citation strength

Prominence isn't just what's on the Google Business Profile itself, it also reflects your website's authority and how consistently your business is listed across the web. A competitor with stronger citations and a more established web presence can out-rank a better-reviewed business on that basis alone. See why NAP consistency matters and the full local SEO checklist for what this actually involves.

Review count and recency still matter, separate from rating

A 4.9 average from 12 reviews doesn't necessarily outrank a 4.3 average from 300 recent ones, volume and recency are weighed alongside the rating itself, not replaced by it. A thin review count, even a glowing one, can simply be read as a less-established profile than a competitor's larger, more active one.

What to actually do about it

Pull up your competitor's full profile side by side with yours, category, attributes, photo count, posting activity, website, and citation presence, not just the star rating. The gap is almost always visible somewhere in that comparison once you're looking at the whole picture instead of the one metric that feels most unfair to be losing on.

A quick way to run the comparison

Search the exact term you expect to rank for, from a location similar to where your typical customer would search, and open both listings side by side. Check: is their category a closer match to the search term than yours? Do they have noticeably more photos or recent posts? Does their website look more established? Each of these is a lever you can actually pull, unlike proximity, which you generally can't change without relocating.

When the gap really is just proximity

If everything else checks out roughly even and your competitor is simply closer to the search origin, there's no ranking trick that reliably overcomes that for every search. In that situation, the more productive move is often focusing effort on searches where you do have a proximity edge, rather than trying to outrank a closer competitor for every single query in the category, since that's a fight the location itself decides, not your profile.
