What Happens to a Trustpilot Review When a Business Rebrands or Is Acquired?

GrowGigs Team

A business changes its name, gets acquired, or merges with another company, and the Trustpilot profile built up over years of reviews now sits under a question mark. Does it carry forward, start fresh, or end up stuck in some ambiguous in-between state nobody actually controls?

Trustpilot ties the profile to the business entity, not just the name

Unlike a simple name change, Trustpilot's profile is generally tied to the underlying business claim rather than the display name alone, meaning a straightforward rebrand with no change in ownership or operations can often update the name on the existing profile while keeping its review history intact. This is the more favorable outcome, and it's the default when nothing about who actually runs the business has changed.

Acquisition changes the calculus

When one company acquires another, whether the acquired business's Trustpilot history survives depends on what actually happens operationally: if the acquired brand keeps operating under its own name with continuity of service, the profile and its reviews typically remain intact. If the acquired business is fully absorbed and stops operating as a distinct entity, its standalone profile may become inactive even though the reviews themselves remain visible as historical record.

Reviews don't automatically merge across two profiles

If a business operated two separate Trustpilot profiles before a merger, say, two formerly independent companies joining under one brand, the review histories do not automatically combine into a single profile. This mirrors the same non-merging behavior seen when Google Business Profiles get consolidated after a merger, and it typically requires a direct request to Trustpilot support to resolve, rather than happening automatically on its own.

What a new owner inherits, and what they don't get to erase

A new owner inherits the existing review history along with the business, for better or worse, in the same way a change in ownership doesn't reset a Google listing's reviews. There is no legitimate path to wipe a profile's history clean simply because new management wants a fresh start; the reviews reflect the business entity's history, not any particular owner's preference about how that history should read.

Does closing and reopening under a new name reset anything?

Closing a business and reopening under a genuinely new legal entity and name is a different scenario from a rebrand, closer to the Google version of this same question. Trustpilot evaluates this case by case rather than offering an automatic reset, and a reopening that's functionally the same business under a new name doesn't guarantee a clean slate just because the paperwork changed.

Update the profile proactively rather than waiting

Once a rebrand or acquisition is final, update the Trustpilot business name, URL, and description promptly through your verified account rather than leaving a stale profile that confuses customers trying to verify which business they're actually reviewing. A profile that lags behind the actual rebrand invites confusion that works against you regardless of how the review history itself is handled.

When to contact Trustpilot support directly

For anything beyond a simple name update, consolidating two profiles, confirming how an acquisition affects an existing listing, or disputing how a closure was categorized, a direct support request with documentation of the actual corporate change gets a more reliable answer than guessing at how the automated system will handle an unusual situation.

Plan for this before the deal closes, not after

If a rebrand or acquisition is already in motion, raise the Trustpilot profile question alongside the other digital-asset items in the transition plan, domain transfers, social accounts, Google Business Profile updates, rather than treating it as an afterthought once the paperwork is signed. A business that updates its listing and clarifies profile status promptly avoids the stretch of confused customers and stale information that otherwise follows a change nobody actually announced anywhere official.
