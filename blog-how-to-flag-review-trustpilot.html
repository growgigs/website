How to Flag a Review on Trustpilot: Grounds, Evidence, and What to Expect

GrowGigs Team

Trustpilot has its own flagging system, separate from Google's, with its own grounds for removal and its own evidence expectations. Here's how the process actually works in practice.

Where flagging happens

Reports go through your Trustpilot Business account, not a public "report" button on the review itself. Log in, open your review inbox, find the review in question, and use the flag option to select the reason and submit your case. Without a business account, you don't have direct access to this flow.

What grounds actually qualify

  • Not based on a genuine experience. The reviewer can't verify they actually interacted with your business.
  • Inaccurate or misleading content. The review states something factually false about the transaction or service.
  • Wrong business. The review is clearly about a different company, sometimes a mix-up with a similarly named business.
  • Abusive content or personal data. Harassment, threats, or private information about an identifiable person.
  • Part of a coordinated pattern. Evidence the review is one of a batch, competitor-driven or otherwise inorganic.

The "not based on a genuine experience" category has an extra step

If you flag a review on these grounds, Trustpilot's process typically requires you to first request more information from the reviewer directly, giving them a short window, commonly a few days, to respond with proof of their experience. If they don't respond or can't substantiate it, that strengthens your case; if they do respond with verifiable details, the review generally stays.

What evidence actually moves a case forward

The strongest evidence ties directly back to your own records: a screenshot from your order system, CRM, or booking platform showing no match for the reviewer's name, email, or any transaction ID they reference. Vague statements like "we don't think this is a real customer" carry far less weight than a concrete records check showing no matching entry at all.

What to expect on timing

Trustpilot generally reviews reports faster when the violation is obvious (clear abuse, explicit personal information) and takes longer for cases requiring more judgment, like disputing whether a genuine transaction occurred. Well-documented reports with clear evidence tend to move faster than vague ones, this is consistent with how most platform moderation works, not unique to Trustpilot.

What happens if your flag is declined

Trustpilot will typically explain why a report didn't result in removal. If the reasoning cites something you can address, more specific evidence, a corrected category, it's worth resubmitting with that gap closed rather than re-flagging the same report unchanged. If the review genuinely reflects a real (if harsh) customer experience, no amount of resubmission will change that outcome, and a public response addressing the concern is the better path at that point.

The honest limits

Trustpilot, like every platform, makes its own determination based on its own policies. Filing a well-documented report with real evidence significantly improves your odds compared to a vague complaint, but nothing guarantees a specific outcome. Genuine, if unflattering, reviews stay up, and that's by design.
