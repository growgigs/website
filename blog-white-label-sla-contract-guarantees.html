What SLAs or Guarantees Should Be in a White-Label Partnership Contract

GrowGigs Team

A white-label vendor's sales deck promises fast turnaround and great results. None of that means anything once a client is actually waiting on a status update and the agency has no contractual language to point to. The SLA is where a partnership either becomes something you can actually run a business on, or stays a handshake.

Response time, not just resolution time

The most useful SLA term isn't how fast a case gets resolved, resolution time varies legitimately by case complexity, it's how fast the vendor acknowledges a new request or a status inquiry. A contract specifying a maximum response window, 24 or 48 business hours, say, gives the agency something concrete to point to with its own client, even on a case that's genuinely still in progress.

Define scope precisely, not aspirationally

  • What's included per case versus what counts as a separate, billable request.
  • Which platforms are covered under the base agreement, and which require a separate rate or aren't offered at all.
  • Volume assumptions, whether the pricing holds at the agency's current case volume or changes past a certain threshold.

Be honest about what a "guarantee" can actually mean

No legitimate vendor can guarantee a specific review gets removed, platforms make that call, not the vendor. What a contract can reasonably guarantee is process: that every eligible case gets filed through the correct channel, that the agency receives a clear explanation when a case doesn't qualify, and that fees are structured around results rather than effort. A written guarantee that promises outcomes a platform controls is itself a red flag, regardless of how confident it sounds, similar to the unverifiable success-rate claims worth scrutinizing before signing.

Reporting cadence as a contractual term, not a courtesy

Put the actual reporting schedule in writing, weekly status, a dashboard, a standing call, rather than leaving it as an informal expectation. This is the single biggest source of friction in white-label relationships: an agency promising its own client visibility it can't actually deliver because the vendor's reporting commitment was never pinned down.

Escalation path for stalled cases

Specify what happens when a case sits past a reasonable window with no movement, who the agency escalates to, and what response that escalation is owed within. A vendor without a defined escalation path is asking the agency to simply wait and trust, which works fine until the one case that actually matters to a client relationship stalls.

Termination and data handoff terms

Include what happens to in-progress cases and historical reporting data if either side ends the relationship. This matters more than it seems at signing time; agencies that skip it discover the gap only when they actually need to transition clients elsewhere, a scenario worth thinking through before it happens rather than during it.

Pricing model and incentive alignment

The SLA terms above only hold up if the underlying pricing structure doesn't quietly undercut them. A vendor paid the same regardless of outcome has less incentive to hit the response and resolution commitments you've written into the contract than one whose compensation is tied to actually delivering.

Who owns communication with the end client during escalation

Decide upfront whether the vendor ever speaks directly with the agency's client during an escalation, even anonymously, or whether every word that reaches the client always routes through the agency first. Neither approach is wrong, but leaving it undecided until a tense case forces the question is how agencies end up with a vendor improvising client-facing language they never approved.

Put it in writing before the first case, not after

These terms matter most before the relationship is tested, not after a bad case reveals a gap. A vendor resistant to committing any of this to writing upfront is telling you something about how the relationship will run once the sales conversation is over and the actual work begins.
