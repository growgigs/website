How to Price a White-Label Local SEO Package When Reselling to Clients

GrowGigs Team

Review removal has a natural pricing model built in, pay per result, since a specific review either comes down or it doesn't. Local SEO has no equivalent finish line, which is exactly why agencies reselling it under their own brand tend to get the pricing structure wrong on the first attempt.

Why local SEO can't be priced like review removal

Local SEO is ongoing optimization and maintenance work, not a discrete, completable task, so a pay-per-result or revenue-share model built for review removal doesn't translate cleanly. The honest pricing conversation for local SEO starts from a retainer, not a per-case fee.

Retainer vs. project-based: pick based on the client's actual need

  • Retainer fits ongoing profile management, posting cadence, review monitoring, and rank tracking, work that never really "finishes."
  • Project-based fits a defined scope like a one-time profile audit and rebuild, category correction, or a NAP consistency cleanup across directories, work with a clear start and end.
  • Many agencies run both: a project fee for the initial cleanup, converting into a retainer for ongoing maintenance once the foundation is in place.

Build in your margin before you quote the client

Decide your markup over the wholesale white-label cost before pricing conversations with the client start, not during them. A common structural mistake is quoting a client price first and reverse-engineering margin after signing the vendor agreement, which leaves no room to adjust if the actual scope turns out larger than estimated.

Bundling with review removal changes the math

Agencies that bundle local SEO with review removal under one retainer often price the bundle below the sum of the two services sold separately, since the bundled client is lower-churn and easier to manage than two separate smaller engagements. Just be careful the bundle discount doesn't eat into margin on the harder-to-scope local SEO component specifically.

Price by market competitiveness, not a flat national rate

A local SEO package for a business in a low-competition market costs meaningfully less to actually execute well than the same package in a dense, highly competitive metro, so a single flat rate across every client either overcharges the easy markets or undercharges the hard ones. Tiering pricing by competitive density, even roughly, protects margin on the harder accounts.

Set expectations about timeline, not just price

Local SEO results compound over months, not weeks, and a client sold on an aggressive timeline will judge the retainer's value against that promise regardless of what was actually realistic. Price the package to reflect a sustained engagement, and set the client's timeline expectations to match, rather than using an aggressive timeline as a sales lever that creates a mismatch later.

Decide what triggers a scope change, before the first renewal

A client that opens a second location, adds a new service line, or asks for review monitoring layered onto the existing local SEO retainer is a scope change, not a favor squeezed into the existing fee. Define in advance what counts as in-scope versus billable as an addition, since this conversation is far easier to have upfront than mid-retainer when a client has already come to expect an expanding scope at a flat price.

Account for the agency's own account-management time

The wholesale execution cost from the white-label vendor isn't the only cost in the retainer; the agency's own time spent managing the client relationship, relaying updates, and handling check-in calls has to be priced in too. Agencies that price purely off the vendor's wholesale rate without accounting for their own overhead tend to discover the margin was thinner than it looked once renewal season arrives.

What this protects, beyond the immediate sale

A pricing structure that accounts for real wholesale cost, market difficulty, and realistic timelines is what keeps a white-label local SEO offering sustainable past the first few clients, rather than profitable only on paper until renewal conversations start.
