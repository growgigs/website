How to Get a Scam Website Impersonating Your Business Taken Down

GrowGigs Team

A site copying your name, logo, or branding to run a scam, phishing for payments, collecting personal information, or selling fake products, is a different problem from a copied paragraph of website text. It moves faster, causes real harm to real people, and the reporting process reflects that urgency.

Confirm what you're dealing with

Look closely at the site: is it impersonating your brand to deceive customers into paying, sharing information, or downloading something, or is it a lower-stakes case of a competitor using similar branding without deceptive intent? The distinction matters because scam/phishing reports get routed and prioritized differently than standard trademark disputes, and misclassifying it slows things down.

Document before it disappears

Scam sites often go up and down quickly, or shift domains once reported. Screenshot the site in full, including the URL, any payment or contact forms, and anything showing your branding being used. Note the exact date and time. If customers have reported being contacted or scammed by the fake site, keep records of those reports too, they strengthen the case significantly.

Report to the hosting provider as a priority

Identify the host through a hosting/IP lookup, and file an abuse report specifically citing fraud, phishing, or brand impersonation, not just a general content complaint. Most hosts treat fraud and phishing reports as higher priority than ordinary content disputes, since hosting active scam infrastructure creates serious liability for them.

Report to the domain registrar

Registrars generally have abuse policies covering fraudulent use of a domain, separate from a host's policies. A WHOIS lookup identifies the registrar even when the registrant's own details are hidden behind privacy protection. Registrar-level action can suspend the domain entirely, which is often more effective than removing content from an active site.

Report to Google Safe Browsing and search engines

Reporting a phishing or scam site to Google's Safe Browsing program can get it flagged with warnings in Chrome and search results, reducing how many people reach it even before the takedown completes. This runs in parallel with, not instead of, the host and registrar reports.

Warn your actual customers

If the scam site is actively targeting people who know your business, a direct notice, on your real website, social channels, or email list, warning that the fake site isn't affiliated with you can limit damage while the takedown process plays out. This doesn't replace the formal reports, but it's often the fastest thing you can do.

When to involve law enforcement

Active financial fraud or phishing targeting your customers can be reported to the FTC and, depending on scale and jurisdiction, local law enforcement or the FBI's IC3. This is worth pursuing alongside the takedown process rather than as a replacement for it, since a hosting or registrar report can move faster than a law enforcement investigation.
