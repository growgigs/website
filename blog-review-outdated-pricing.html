How to Handle a Negative Review That Cites Pricing You No Longer Charge

GrowGigs Team

A two-star review complains about a price that was accurate when the reviewer visited two years ago but has since changed, and a prospective customer reading it today has no way of knowing the number is stale. The review wasn't false when it was posted, which makes this a genuinely different problem than a fabricated claim, and it calls for a different kind of response.

Why outdated information isn't the same as false information

A review citing a price you charged at the time is an accurate historical account, not a fabrication, which means it almost never qualifies for removal under a platform's false-claim or misleading-content policy the way a genuinely invented complaint would. Platforms generally treat a review as a snapshot of the reviewer's actual experience at the time they had it, not an obligation on them to keep checking whether your pricing has since changed.

Why removal is the wrong first tool here

  • The review was accurate when posted, so a standard removal report citing false information will typically get rejected, since nothing in it was untrue at the time.
  • Platforms don't police currency, only accuracy at posting. There's no policy mechanism requiring a review to reflect your current pricing structure months or years later.
  • A removal attempt here can look like you're disputing a review you simply dislike, rather than one that's genuinely false, which can hurt your credibility on future, more legitimate reports.

What actually works instead: a dated public response

Most platforms let the business reply publicly underneath a review, and that's the right tool here, a brief, factual note that your pricing has changed since this review was posted, with the current structure or a link to it. This gives every future reader the accurate current information directly alongside the outdated complaint, without requiring the platform to remove anything at all.

Date your response so it ages well too

Include the date of your reply in the response itself, since a response that simply says "our pricing has changed" will itself eventually look stale to a reader five years from now. A dated note, "as of [date], our current pricing is X," stays useful and credible even as your prices continue to shift over time, rather than becoming its own outdated artifact sitting next to the original review.

How this differs from other categories you can actually report

This is a different situation than a review making a false factual claim or one posted on the wrong business profile entirely, both of which involve content that was inaccurate from the moment it was posted. An outdated-pricing review was true once, it just hasn't aged well, and treating it as a reportable falsehood misunderstands what the platform's policy is actually designed to catch.

Consider whether the volume of these reviews signals a bigger problem

If outdated-pricing complaints keep surfacing repeatedly, it may be worth reviewing how clearly your current pricing is displayed across your own website and listings, since a prospective customer who can't easily find current numbers may be more inclined to trust an old, cited figure over nothing at all. Making current pricing easy to find reduces how much weight any single outdated review can carry with someone actively comparing options.

Keep your public response policy consistent

Decide in advance how you'll handle every pricing-related review that cites a number you no longer charge, rather than drafting a one-off reply each time a new one surfaces, so your responses read as a consistent practice rather than a scattered, reactive pattern that looks different depending on who's watching. A clear, repeatable approach also makes it genuinely easier to train anyone else on your team who ends up responding to reviews on your behalf.
