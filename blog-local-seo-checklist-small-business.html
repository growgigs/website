Local SEO Checklist for Small Businesses

GrowGigs Team

Local SEO involves a lot of moving pieces, but most small businesses aren't missing everything, they're missing one or two fundamentals. Here's a condensed checklist to find the gaps.

Google Business Profile

  • Profile fully verified, with accurate name, category, hours, and service area.
  • At least a handful of current photos, exterior, interior, team, or work in progress.
  • Every review responded to, not just the negative ones.
  • Posts or updates published periodically rather than a dormant, untouched profile.

Website fundamentals

  • Your city or service area mentioned naturally in page titles and content, not stuffed in unnaturally.
  • A dedicated page per service or location if you serve multiple areas, rather than one page trying to cover everything.
  • Site loads reasonably fast and displays correctly on mobile, a large share of local searches happen on phones.
  • Contact information (name, address, phone) visible on the page itself, not just buried in an image or footer.

NAP consistency

  • Your business Name, Address, and Phone number match exactly across your website, Google Business Profile, and any directories or citation sites you're listed on.
  • Old addresses or phone numbers from a past move are updated everywhere, not just on your primary listing.

Reviews

  • A steady, ongoing flow of new reviews rather than a pile from years ago and nothing recent.
  • A simple, low-friction way for satisfied customers to leave a review (a direct link works better than asking them to search for you).
  • A plan for addressing reviews that don't reflect genuine issues, without resorting to anything that violates platform policy.

Citations and directories

  • Listed accurately on the major relevant directories for your industry, not just Google.
  • No duplicate or outdated listings competing with your current one, duplicates can dilute and confuse local signals.

What to do with this list

Go through it item by item rather than assuming you're covered because you handled the big obvious things. Most local ranking problems trace back to something small and fixable on this list, an unverified profile field, an inconsistent address somewhere, or a review section that's gone quiet, not some complex technical issue.
