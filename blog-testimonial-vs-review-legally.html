What's the Difference Between a Testimonial and a Review, Legally?

GrowGigs Team

A glowing quote on your homepage and a five-star review on Google both say nice things about your business, but they're governed by genuinely different rules, controlled by different parties, and carry different risks if handled carelessly. Businesses that treat the two as interchangeable tend to get the compliance side wrong.

Who controls the content is the core distinction

A testimonial is content you, the business, collect and publish yourself, typically a quote you selected, edited for length, and placed on your own website or marketing material. A review lives on a third-party platform, Google, Trustpilot, Yelp, under that platform's own policies, and you have no editorial control over what it says once it's posted.

Why this distinction isn't just academic

A business owner who treats the two as functionally the same tends to assume the same freedoms and the same obligations apply to both, which leads to real mistakes on both sides, editing a testimonial so heavily it misrepresents what the customer actually said, or assuming a bad review can simply be edited or quietly removed from your own site the way a testimonial can. Understanding which rules actually apply to which type of content avoids both kinds of error before they happen.

Why this distinction matters legally

  • Testimonials fall under advertising and endorsement regulations in most jurisdictions, requiring disclosure if there's any material connection, payment, free product, employment, between the business and the person quoted.
  • Reviews fall under the hosting platform's own content policies, your ability to influence or remove one runs through that platform's reporting process, not through editorial control the way a testimonial does.
  • Editing is treated completely differently. You can edit a testimonial for length or clarity as long as you don't misrepresent its substance; editing a review's actual text yourself isn't something you can do at all, only the reviewer or the platform can.

The disclosure obligation businesses often miss

If a testimonial comes from someone with a material connection to your business, a paid influencer, an employee, a friend who got a discount, that connection generally needs disclosure under advertising regulations in most jurisdictions that take this seriously. A genuine, unprompted Google review doesn't carry this same disclosure requirement, since the platform's own policies already govern authenticity on their end.

Fabricating either one is a serious problem, just a different kind

A fabricated testimonial, a quote from someone who doesn't exist or never said it, is a direct advertising-law violation your business is fully responsible for since you wrote and published it yourself. A fake review posted by someone impersonating a customer is a platform violation you can report, but importantly, one you're not the author of and don't bear the same direct liability for.

Why the terminology confusion causes real problems

Businesses sometimes describe a testimonial as a "review" in marketing copy, implying third-party platform verification that doesn't actually exist behind a hand-picked quote you control entirely. This isn't just sloppy language, it can read as misleading advertising if a customer reasonably understood "review" to mean independently verified third-party feedback rather than content you selected and published yourself.

Use both, but label them accurately

Testimonials and genuine third-party reviews serve complementary purposes, curated quotes for your own marketing, platform reviews for independent social proof, but accurately representing which is which protects you from an avoidable compliance problem and keeps your marketing honest about where the praise is actually coming from.

Review widgets blur the line, but don't erase it

A widget pulling live reviews from Google or Trustpilot directly onto your website displays genuine third-party content, not a testimonial, even though it appears on your own site in a similar visual spot. The distinction still holds: you didn't write or select that specific text, the platform's own policies govern it, and you can't individually edit or remove entries the way you could with a hand-picked testimonial quote you control outright.
