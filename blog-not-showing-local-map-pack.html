Why Isn't My Business Showing Up in the Local Map Pack?

GrowGigs Team

The local 3-pack is the small map box with three businesses that appears above regular search results for local queries. Missing from it is frustrating, and usually comes down to a handful of common, fixable blockers.

Your profile isn't fully verified or set up

An unverified Google Business Profile, or one missing basic information like category, hours, or service area, is often disqualified from the map pack entirely regardless of everything else. This is the first thing worth confirming, it sounds obvious but gets overlooked more than you'd expect, especially for businesses that set up a profile years ago and never revisited it.

Proximity to the searcher

Local ranking is heavily influenced by how close your business is to the person searching or the location implied in their query. A business three miles from the searcher will often outrank a stronger, more optimized business ten miles away for that specific search. There's no fix for this beyond a physical location or service area change, it's simply part of how local search works.

Category mismatch

If your primary Google Business Profile category doesn't closely match what people are actually searching for, you may be invisible for those searches even with a strong profile otherwise. A general "contractor" category won't compete well against specifically categorized "roofing contractor" listings for roofing searches.

Review volume and recency relative to competitors

The businesses appearing in the map pack usually have a meaningful volume of recent reviews. If competitors in your area have significantly more or more recent reviews, that's a real disadvantage, not because reviews alone control ranking, but because review activity is one of several signals working together.

Inconsistent business information across the web

If your name, address, or phone number differs between your website, your Business Profile, and directory listings, that inconsistency can undermine how confidently Google associates all those signals with the same business. Auditing and fixing this across your major listings is a worthwhile, one-time cleanup.

Genuine competition

Sometimes the honest answer is that the map pack for a given search has three spots and your market has fifteen strong competitors. Optimization improves your odds and fixes self-inflicted problems, but it doesn't create map pack spots that don't exist. In highly competitive categories, consistent incremental improvement matters more than any single fix.

Where to start

Confirm verification and profile completeness first, since that can be an outright disqualifier. Then check category accuracy and NAP consistency, both are within your direct control. Review volume takes longer to build but compounds over time.
