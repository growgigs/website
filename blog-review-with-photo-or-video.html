Can You Remove a Review That Includes a Photo or Video?

GrowGigs Team

A one-star review is frustrating on its own. One with a photo of a dirty table or a video of a bad interaction feels harder to argue with, and harder to get removed. The policy rules are mostly the same either way, but the evidence picture changes in a few important ways.

The removal policy itself doesn't change

A photo or video attached to a review doesn't create a new category of violation, or a new exemption from one. The same underlying question applies: does the review, media included, violate platform content policy, fake or spam content, a non-customer, harassment, off-topic material. Media is part of the review's content, evaluated the same way the written portion is.

Where media actually changes the analysis

A photo or video can work for or against a removal case, depending on what it shows. Media that clearly doesn't match your business, the wrong storefront, a product you don't carry, a location that isn't yours, is strong supporting evidence for a wrong-business or fake-review report. Media that's genuinely from your business showing a real problem makes a removal case significantly harder, since it's documented evidence the experience happened, not just an assertion.

Fabricated or manipulated media

Photos and video can be staged, taken somewhere else entirely, or edited. If you have reason to believe a photo or video attached to a review doesn't actually depict your business, genuinely wasn't taken there, is from a different location, is reused from another business, or has been altered, that's a legitimate basis to report the review as fake or misleading, the same as any other fabricated content.

What doesn't qualify as grounds for removal

A real photo of a real problem at your business doesn't become removable just because it's embarrassing or because you wish it weren't public. Platforms generally don't entertain "please remove this photo, it makes us look bad" as grounds on its own, the same honest limit that applies to text-only genuine reviews.

What to actually check before reporting

  • Does the image or video location/details match your business? Inconsistencies (wrong décor, wrong menu, wrong signage) support a wrong-business or fake claim.
  • Does the reviewer's account or review history show a pattern? The same account-level signals that apply to any fake review check still apply here.
  • Is there metadata or context suggesting the media is reused or staged? A reverse image search can sometimes reveal media pulled from elsewhere online.

If the media is genuine and the review stays up

A real photo of a real issue, left up because it's accurate, is still worth a thoughtful public response. Acknowledging what the image shows directly, rather than ignoring it, generally reads better to anyone scrolling through than a reply that avoids the obvious.

Why visual reviews carry more weight with readers

A photo or video attached to a review tends to get more attention from anyone browsing your profile than a text-only one, both the good and the bad. This cuts both ways: a glowing review with a great photo does real work for you, and a damaging one with visual evidence does real work against you. It's part of why distinguishing fake from genuine matters even more here than with text alone, since a fabricated image that goes unreported can sit at the top of your profile doing outsized damage.

What platforms generally require to act on disputed media

If you're reporting a photo or video as fabricated or misleading, be specific about what's wrong with it rather than making a general claim. Pointing to a concrete mismatch, a storefront that doesn't match your actual location, a product you've never carried, signage in a different language than what you use, gives the platform something it can actually verify, which moves a report further than a vague "this isn't real" assertion.
