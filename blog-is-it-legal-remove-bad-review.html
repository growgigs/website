Is It Legal to Have a Bad Review Removed?

GrowGigs Team

Business owners ask this more often than you'd expect, usually a little sheepishly, as if requesting removal is inherently a gray area. It isn't. The legality question has a straightforward answer once you separate what's actually being requested from what sometimes gets confused with it.

The short answer

Yes, it's legal to request that a platform remove a review, and it's legal for the platform to do so, when the review violates that platform's own published policies. Google, Yelp, and other review platforms explicitly built reporting and removal processes into their systems precisely because they expect businesses to use them. Using the process the platform provides isn't a loophole, it's the intended mechanism.

What actually raises legal or ethical questions

  • Posting fake positive reviews to offset a negative one. This violates platform policy and, in some jurisdictions, consumer protection law (the FTC has taken action on fake reviews in the US).
  • Threatening or pressuring a genuine reviewer to delete their review. This can cross into harassment or, depending on the specifics, legal exposure for the business, not just a policy violation.
  • Paying a third party to fraudulently impersonate the platform or the reviewer to force removal. This is a different, and clearly illegal, category from requesting removal through the platform's own legitimate process.

None of that describes flagging a review that violates a stated policy and asking the platform to act on it. That's a normal, sanctioned use of tools the platform itself built.

Why the hesitation exists anyway

Part of it is a general discomfort with anything that looks like "managing" public feedback, as though a business should simply absorb whatever gets posted. But platforms themselves don't operate that way, they draw a line between legitimate criticism (protected, stays up) and content that violates policy (fake reviews, harassment, off-topic content, conflicts of interest), and they built a reporting system specifically to enforce that line.

The line that actually matters: genuine versus not

The legitimate version of this process only works for content that genuinely violates a policy, a fake review, one from a non-customer, one containing harassment or personal information. It's not a tool for making a real customer's honest complaint disappear because it's unflattering. Reporting a genuine, policy-compliant negative review and hoping it gets removed anyway wastes time and, if a business does this repeatedly or dishonestly, can affect how platforms weigh that business's future reports.

What "guaranteed removal" claims should tell you

Because platforms make the final determination based on whether content actually violates their policy, nobody, including a business or any third party helping them, can guarantee a specific review will come down. A legitimate reporting process improves your odds significantly when a genuine violation exists; it doesn't override the platform's judgment. Any service claiming a guarantee is either overpromising or planning to do something outside the platform's legitimate process, which is where the real legal risk actually lives.
