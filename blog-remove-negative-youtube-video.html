How to Remove a Negative Video About Your Business From YouTube

GrowGigs Team

A video attacking your business behaves differently from a written review: it can combine footage, commentary, and claims in ways that are harder to dispute piece by piece, and it tends to rank well in both YouTube's and Google's search results. The removal path depends entirely on what the video actually contains.

Start by identifying what kind of problem it actually is

YouTube doesn't have a single "remove bad video about my business" button, because different videos violate different policies, or none at all. A video using your copyrighted footage or product photos is a copyright issue. A video making false factual claims presented as fact is a defamation issue. A video using your logo or trademark in a misleading way is a trademark issue. Harassment, threats, or doxxing of you or your staff falls under YouTube's harassment and cyberbullying policies. Get this categorization right first, since each has its own report form and its own standard of proof.

If it uses your copyrighted material

File a copyright takedown through YouTube's copyright report tool, not the general flagging option. You'll need to identify the specific copyrighted material (your footage, photos, or other original content), where it appears in the video by timestamp, and confirm you're the rights holder. This is the most clear-cut path when it applies, since it doesn't require proving anything about the surrounding commentary, only that unauthorized copyrighted material is present.

If it makes false factual claims

Legal opinion and harsh criticism are protected and won't come down through a report, no matter how unfair they feel. A specific, checkable factual claim that's false (a claim you were sued when you weren't, a fabricated quote, a fake incident that never happened) is different. Document exactly which statements are false and why, with evidence, before filing anything. This category has a higher bar than copyright and often takes longer to resolve, since YouTube has to weigh the claim against the right to publish critical commentary.

If it's harassment rather than criticism of the business

  • Threats or targeted harassment of you or employees by name, rather than criticism of the business itself, falls under YouTube's harassment policy and can be reported directly.
  • Personal information exposed (home address, personal phone number, family details) in the video is a privacy violation with its own report path, separate from the business dispute itself.
  • Repeated videos targeting the same individual across a channel, rather than a single critical video, can qualify as a harassment pattern even if no single video crosses the line alone.

What to expect after filing

YouTube's review timelines vary by category, copyright claims typically move fastest, policy violations like harassment take longer since they require human review of context. A video that doesn't violate any policy, even one that's damaging and unfair, generally won't come down through a report, and pursuing that kind of case usually means a direct response (your own video, a statement, or outreach to the creator) rather than relying on takedown tools that don't apply.

Why this differs from a written review complaint

A written review is usually a single, contained claim you can quote and address directly. A video can combine several different issues at once, a few seconds of unauthorized footage, a misleading title, and a genuinely false claim somewhere in the middle, each of which falls under a different policy and a different report path. Watch the full video before filing anything, and note timestamps for each specific issue rather than reporting the video as a whole as "defamatory" or "unfair." A report that identifies the specific violating segment moves faster than a general complaint about tone.

If the video keeps ranking even after a partial resolution

Sometimes a takedown succeeds on one piece (the copyrighted footage gets removed, for example) but the video itself stays up with the rest of its content intact, still ranking for searches that include your business name. In that case, the realistic path forward is usually a combination of continuing to pursue any remaining valid claims and building out other content, case studies, press, your own response, that gives search results something more current and more favorable to rank alongside it.
