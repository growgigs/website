Can a Trustpilot Review Be Removed If It Was Incentivized by a Competitor's Payment or Offer?

GrowGigs Team

A one-star review shows up, and then evidence surfaces later, a screenshot, a forum post, a direct message, showing a competitor actually paid or offered something to the reviewer in exchange for posting it. This is a distinct, more serious violation than a simple conflict of interest, and it changes what evidence you actually need to build a successful case.

Why this is stronger than a standard conflict-of-interest claim

A regular conflict-of-interest case relies on showing the reviewer has some connection to a competitor, employment, ownership, an incentive case goes further: it shows the review itself was manufactured as a transaction, paid or compensated speech rather than a genuine opinion, regardless of any connection. Trustpilot's guidelines treat incentivized reviews, paid or otherwise compensated, as a direct violation independent of the conflict-of-interest category.

What actually counts as an incentive

  • Direct payment for posting a negative review, the clearest and most serious version.
  • A product, service, or discount offered in exchange for the review, even if framed informally rather than as a direct cash transaction.
  • Reciprocal arrangements, two competing businesses agreeing to review-bomb each other, which is still an incentive arrangement even without money changing hands directly.

Where this evidence actually comes from

Incentivized-review evidence rarely surfaces through the review itself, it comes from outside sources: a screenshot of a direct message offering payment, a forum or group chat where the arrangement was discussed, or occasionally a disgruntled party to the original arrangement who comes forward later with the full story. This is why incentivized-review cases often start from a tip or an accidental leak rather than any careful analysis of the review's own text.

Verify the evidence carefully before relying on it

A screenshot can be edited, so corroborate it where possible, consistent details across multiple independent pieces of evidence, a source willing to confirm it directly, timestamps that line up with the review's actual posting date. Trustpilot's moderators will scrutinize screenshot evidence given how easily it can be fabricated, so the strength of your corroboration matters just as much as having the screenshot itself in hand.

How to actually file this report

Cite the specific policy against incentivized or paid reviews directly, rather than filing under the general conflict-of-interest category, since the evidence and standard genuinely differ in meaningful ways. Present the corroborating evidence clearly and in order, don't just assert an incentive existed, show exactly what demonstrates it.

Consider whether this warrants more than a platform report

A documented, deliberate scheme to manufacture fake negative reviews against a competitor can constitute tortious interference or unfair competition in some jurisdictions, beyond whatever Trustpilot itself does with the report. If the scheme is well-documented and the harm is genuinely significant, this is worth raising with legal counsel alongside the platform report, not as a substitute for filing it in the first place.

Protect yourself from the same accusation

Never offer anything, payment, discounts, favors, in exchange for a review of any kind, positive or negative, about a competitor or your own business. The same conduct that makes a competitor's incentivized review against you removable would make your own team's incentivized reviews equally vulnerable if the roles were ever reversed.

Train staff on what not to say, even informally

A well-meaning employee offering an unhappy customer a discount "if you take that review down" can accidentally create the same incentivized-content problem in reverse, now directed at your own positive outcome rather than against a competitor. Make clear to anyone customer-facing that discussing reviews and compensation in the same conversation at all, in either direction, is off-limits, regardless of the intention behind it.
