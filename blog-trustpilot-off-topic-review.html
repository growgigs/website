Can You Get a Trustpilot Review Removed for Being Off-Topic?

GrowGigs Team

A one-star review complaining about a delayed courier, a payment processor's fee, or a manufacturer's product defect, none of it actually about the business the review is posted under. Off-topic is one of Trustpilot's clearer removal categories, but "off-topic" means something narrower than most businesses initially assume.

What "off-topic" actually means on Trustpilot

Trustpilot's guidelines require a review to relate to a genuine experience with the specific business it's posted against. A review can be removed as off-topic when it's substantively about a different company entirely, a shipping carrier, a payment gateway, a supplier several steps removed from the actual transaction, rather than the reviewed business itself.

Common off-topic scenarios that actually qualify

  • Courier or shipping complaints directed at the delivery company's handling, not anything the business itself controlled.
  • Payment processor issues where the complaint is really about a third-party checkout or billing system.
  • Manufacturer defects on a product the business merely sold or distributed but didn't make.
  • Reviews meant for a different business entirely, posted on the wrong profile by mistake, more common than it sounds with similarly named companies.

What doesn't qualify, even if it feels off-topic

A review criticizing your own customer service response to someone else's problem, say, how you personally handled a courier delay, is actually about you now, even though the original complaint started elsewhere. Trustpilot draws this distinction carefully: if your business's own handling of the situation becomes part of the complaint, the review is no longer purely off-topic, regardless of what triggered it.

Build the case around the actual transaction chain

The strongest off-topic reports clearly map out who did what: what you provided, what the third party controlled, and where the reviewer's complaint actually lands in that chain. A vague assertion that "this isn't really about us" is weaker than a report that walks Trustpilot through the specific handoff point where responsibility shifted to someone else.

How this differs from a "not a genuine experience" report

Off-topic and non-genuine-experience are separate categories addressing different problems, off-topic concerns what the review is actually about, while genuine-experience reports concern whether a real transaction with your business happened at all. A review can fail on either ground independently, so it's worth identifying which one actually applies before filing, since citing the wrong category weakens an otherwise valid report.

What to expect from the review itself

Off-topic reviews often read as confused or misdirected rather than deliberately malicious, the reviewer genuinely believed they were addressing the right party. That doesn't change the outcome you're pursuing, but it's worth keeping tone measured in your own report rather than treating a likely honest mistake as bad faith, since Trustpilot evaluates the substance of the complaint either way.

When the line is genuinely unclear

Some cases sit in real gray area, a reviewer upset about a bundled service where your business and a partner each handled part of the experience. In those cases, file based on the strongest, most clearly supportable category available rather than forcing an off-topic claim that only partially fits, and be prepared for Trustpilot to evaluate it under whichever standard it actually falls under.

Document the handoff point before you file

Gather whatever evidence actually shows where your business's involvement ended and the third party's began, order confirmations, shipping records, correspondence showing you flagged the issue to the responsible party at the time. A report backed by a clear paper trail of where responsibility shifted carries more weight than an assertion alone, and it gives Trustpilot's reviewers something concrete to evaluate rather than two competing accounts of who was actually responsible for the outcome.
