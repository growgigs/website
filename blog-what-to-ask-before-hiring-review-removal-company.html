What to Ask Before Hiring a Review Removal Company

GrowGigs Team

This space attracts its share of overpromising and outright scams, alongside legitimate providers doing real work. A handful of direct questions separates one from the other quickly.

"How do you actually get reviews removed?"

A legitimate provider can explain their process in plain terms: identifying which platform policy a review violates, filing a report citing that policy with supporting evidence, and following up through the platform's own channels. If the answer is vague, or leans on phrases like "proprietary methods" or "our connections," that's worth pressing on, there's no secret backchannel to Google, Yelp, or any major platform.

"What happens if the review doesn't qualify for removal?"

This is the single most revealing question. A provider who says "everything can be removed" or won't give you a straight answer is telling you something important. A legitimate provider will say plainly that not every review qualifies, genuine negative experiences generally aren't removable, and will tell you honestly when a review doesn't meet the bar rather than taking your money and trying anyway.

"How am I billed if it doesn't come down?"

Pay-per-result pricing, where you only pay once a review is actually removed, is the structure that protects you here. If a provider wants a large fee upfront regardless of outcome, on a service where the entire value is the outcome, that's a meaningful risk shift onto you. It's fair to ask this directly and get a clear answer before agreeing to anything.

"Can you guarantee this review comes down?"

No legitimate provider can say yes to this. Platforms make the final determination based on their own policies, not on anything a third party promises. "Guaranteed removal" is one of the clearest red flags in this industry, it means either the provider is overpromising something outside their control, or they're planning to do something outside the platform's legitimate process to force the outcome, which creates real risk for you.

Other red flags worth watching for

  • Pressure to decide immediately. Legitimate review-removal work isn't time-sensitive in a way that requires an on-the-spot decision.
  • No clear explanation of which specific policy a review violates. If they can't tell you why a given review qualifies, they likely haven't actually evaluated it.
  • Reluctance to put pricing or terms in writing. A legitimate provider will document what you're paying for and under what conditions.
  • Claims of a special relationship with Google or the platform. No legitimate provider has an insider fast-track; everyone uses the same reporting channels.

What a reasonable answer sounds like

Something close to: "We'll review the specific content, tell you honestly whether it looks like it violates a policy, and if so, file a report citing that policy with the evidence we can gather. You're billed only if it's actually removed. We can't guarantee it, since that's ultimately the platform's call, but we'll be direct with you about the odds based on what we're seeing." That combination, honesty about limits, a real process, and outcome-based billing, is what separates a legitimate service from the rest.
