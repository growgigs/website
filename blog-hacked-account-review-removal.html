Can a Review Be Removed If the Reviewer's Account Was Hacked or Taken Over?

GrowGigs Team

A negative review appears from an account that genuinely wasn't under its owner's control at the time, compromised through a phishing attempt, a reused password breach, or a stolen session, and the actual person behind that name never wrote a word of it. This is a narrower and more specific scenario than a simple fake or anonymous review, and it has its own path to removal if you can actually establish what happened.

Why account compromise is a distinct category

A fake review comes from an account the poster genuinely controls, used specifically to post something false or policy-violating. An account-takeover review is different in kind, the account itself is real and may even have a genuine purchase history with your business, but whoever actually typed the review wasn't the account's legitimate owner at that moment. That distinction matters because the platform's removal path depends heavily on which scenario you're actually describing.

What actually counts as evidence of a takeover

  • A statement from the account owner confirming they didn't post it, ideally describing roughly when they noticed unauthorized access or a related security alert from their own provider.
  • A timeline that doesn't fit the owner's known behavior, a review posted while the account owner's own records show they were unreachable, hospitalized, or otherwise demonstrably not active online.
  • Content clearly inconsistent with the actual account owner, referencing a business, location, or experience the real account owner has no connection to at all.

Why this is genuinely hard to prove from outside the account

You, as the business being reviewed, have no visibility into someone else's account security, you can't see their login history or confirm a breach occurred, so your removal request almost always depends on getting the actual account owner to confirm the compromise directly to the platform. Without that cooperation, a takeover claim can look indistinguishable from an ordinary dispute over a genuine review, which platforms are far more reluctant to act on.

The account owner's cooperation is usually the deciding factor

If you can identify and reach the real account owner, perhaps because they're an actual past customer who's horrified to learn their account posted something they never wrote, their direct confirmation to the platform carries far more weight than anything you can submit on your own. Platforms are built to trust the account holder's own statement about their own account over a third party's claim, so securing that cooperation is usually the single most useful thing you can do.

How this differs from other suspicious-account scenarios already covered

This is a different situation than a reviewer posting under a fabricated name or an anonymous or already-deleted account, both of which involve a poster who fully controlled the account they used. A genuine takeover means the real owner's identity and the actual author are two different people entirely, which is a narrower claim but, when properly documented, often a stronger one than disputing a review's truthfulness alone.

What to do while you're gathering evidence

Report the review through the platform's standard process in the meantime, noting the suspected compromise, since platforms sometimes flag genuinely suspicious account activity on their own independent of anything you submit. Don't wait for a complete case before reporting at all, a preliminary report plus a later follow-up with the account owner's confirmation is a perfectly normal sequence and often how these cases actually get resolved.

Don't assume every unlikely review is a takeover

A genuinely harsh or out-of-character review isn't, on its own, evidence of account compromise, that's simply an unhappy customer whose review you may dislike but who's entirely within their rights to post it. Reserve the takeover claim for cases where you have an actual, specific reason to believe the account itself was compromised, not just a review that feels unfair or implausible to you.
