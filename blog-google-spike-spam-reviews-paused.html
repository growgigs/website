Google Detected a Spike in Spam Reviews on My Business — What This Means

GrowGigs Team

Some business owners have started receiving a specific notification from Google: a spike in spam reviews was detected, those reviews were removed, and new reviews and ratings are temporarily paused on the listing. Here's what's actually going on and what to do about it.

What's been happening

Reports of this have surged industry-wide, businesses seeing existing reviews vanish, sometimes a large share of them at once, followed by an inability to collect new reviews for a period of time. Google has acknowledged investigating widespread reports of missing reviews on Business Profiles and has confirmed it is pausing new review activity on some affected listings while it works through the issue.

The pattern behind it

A recurring pattern reported by people tracking this closely: a listing gets hit with a burst of fake or spam reviews, or the business itself reports a fake review, and shortly after, the entire profile's reviews disappear and new ones stop posting. The trigger appears tied to how Google's spam detection responds, once suspicious activity is flagged on a profile, the system can pause the whole listing's reviews rather than acting on just the specific review that triggered it.

What Google has said about it

A Google spokesperson has explained that when its systems detect suspicious review activity, the response can include removing reviews and pausing a profile's ability to collect new ones, and that any reviews removed in error will be restored. The notification businesses are receiving states plainly that a spike in spam reviews was detected, those reviews were removed to protect the profile, and new contributions are paused until the risk is mitigated.

How long the pause typically lasts

Google has indicated the pause lifts once the risk is considered mitigated, with businesses generally seeing it resolve within a matter of days rather than being permanent. That said, timelines vary by case, and there's no fixed guarantee on exactly when a specific listing will be cleared.

What to actually do if this happens to you

  • Don't panic-post a flood of new reviews once the pause lifts. A sudden burst of new reviews right after a spam flag can look like exactly the pattern that triggered the pause in the first place.
  • Document what you're seeing. Screenshot your review count and any specific reviews you notice missing, in case you need to reference them later.
  • Check Google Business Profile Support and community forums. This has been a widely reported, actively tracked issue, and checking whether others are seeing the same pattern helps confirm it's platform-side rather than something specific to your listing.
  • Wait rather than repeatedly contest it. Since this is generally described as an automated, temporary protective measure rather than a manual decision against your business specifically, repeated support escalations often can't accelerate it much faster than the system resolving on its own.

Why this happens at the platform level, not because of anything you did wrong

This isn't a penalty against your specific business, it's Google's spam-detection system reacting to activity patterns it considers suspicious, sometimes triggered by bad actors targeting a listing (competitor spam, review-bombing) rather than anything the business itself did. If you believe fake reviews were the actual trigger, that's consistent with what's been reported, the system flagging the whole profile as a side effect of catching the spam.

The bigger picture

Platform-side anti-spam measures, even when they cause temporary friction like this, exist because fake and spam reviews are a real, ongoing problem for local search generally. A short pause while a spike gets sorted out is a different situation from a genuine, permanent removal of your reviews, and for most businesses reporting this issue, normal review activity has resumed once the investigation period passed.
