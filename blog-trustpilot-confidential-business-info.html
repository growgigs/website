Can a Trustpilot Review Be Flagged for Containing Confidential Business Information?

GrowGigs Team

A former client or vendor posts a detailed Trustpilot review that quotes actual contract pricing, internal process details, or terms covered by a signed confidentiality agreement, content that goes well beyond an ordinary complaint about service quality. This is a genuinely different flag than a privacy complaint about a customer's personal information, and it has its own specific requirements before Trustpilot will act on it.

Why confidential business information is its own category

A privacy complaint protects an individual's personal details, their health, financial, or identifying information. A confidential-business-information flag protects your company's own trade secrets, contract terms, internal pricing, or proprietary processes, information that belongs to the business itself rather than to a specific person. Trustpilot's content policies address both, but they're separate grounds requiring separate evidence, and conflating them in a single report tends to weaken both arguments.

What actually counts as confidential business information

  • Specific contract terms or pricing that aren't publicly disclosed anywhere on your own site or marketing materials.
  • Internal processes or proprietary methods a reviewer only had access to through an employment or vendor relationship, not through anything a regular customer would see.
  • Content explicitly covered by a signed NDA or confidentiality clause, where the reviewer had a documented, pre-existing obligation not to disclose it.

The evidence requirement is genuinely strict here

Simply asserting that a review contains "confidential" information isn't enough, Trustpilot generally expects you to show the actual agreement or policy that made the information confidential in the first place, not just your own characterization that it shouldn't have been shared. A signed NDA, an employment confidentiality clause, or a vendor contract's non-disclosure terms are the kind of documentation that actually moves this request forward.

Public pricing doesn't qualify, even if you'd rather it weren't repeated

If the pricing or terms a reviewer quotes are already published on your own website or available to any prospective customer who asks, repeating that same public information in a review isn't a confidentiality violation no matter how unflattering the context. This flag exists for information that was genuinely private and improperly disclosed, not for public information you simply wish weren't highlighted next to a negative experience.

How this differs from the personal-privacy flag already covered

This is a distinct category from a review disclosing a customer's private or medical information, which protects an individual's personal data rather than your company's business information. The two flags can occasionally overlap in a single review, a vendor disclosing both a client's medical detail and your internal pricing in the same post, but each element needs to be evaluated and reported against its own specific policy ground.

Who's actually likely to post this kind of review

This scenario shows up most often with former employees, contractors, or vendors who had legitimate access to confidential information during the relationship and later used it in a public review, rather than ordinary customers who'd have no access to this kind of detail in the first place. Identifying the reviewer's actual relationship to your business, and whether that relationship came with a signed confidentiality obligation, is usually the first step in building a credible report.

Document the disclosure precisely before reporting it

Screenshot the specific confidential content before reporting, and in your report, quote the exact language alongside the specific confidentiality clause it violates rather than describing the review in general terms. A report that draws a direct, specific line between a disclosed detail and the agreement that protected it gives Trustpilot's moderators something concrete to act on, far more than a general complaint that a former insider "said too much."
