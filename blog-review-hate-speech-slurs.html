Can a Google Review Be Removed for Containing Hate Speech or Slurs?

GrowGigs Team

A one-star review that's less about the actual experience and more a vehicle for a slur directed at the business owner's race, religion, gender, or another protected characteristic. This is one of the more clear-cut violations a review can commit, and it's worth knowing it's a distinct category from the general content disputes most removal cases involve.

Why hate speech is its own category

Google's content policies explicitly prohibit hate speech, content that promotes violence or hatred against individuals or groups based on protected attributes, as a standalone violation, separate from the "genuine experience" standard most reviews get evaluated under. This matters because a review can fail this test even if the underlying complaint about your service is entirely legitimate, the hateful language itself is the violation, independent of whether a real transaction occurred.

What actually qualifies

  • Slurs or derogatory terms targeting race, ethnicity, religion, nationality, gender, sexual orientation, disability, or other protected characteristics.
  • Language promoting hatred or violence against a group, even when framed as commentary on your business.
  • Stereotyping language used to demean the business owner or staff based on a protected characteristic, even without an explicit slur.

What doesn't qualify, even when it's ugly

Harsh, rude, or insulting language that doesn't target a protected characteristic, calling service "incompetent" or a business "a scam," however unfair, generally isn't hate speech under this specific policy, even if it's genuinely unpleasant to read. The same honest standard applies here that applies everywhere else: a review being mean or unfair doesn't on its own make it removable, the violation has to be the specific protected-characteristic targeting, not general hostility.

This can overlap with a fake or non-genuine review

Reviews weaponizing hate speech are sometimes also fake reviews from someone with no real transaction history, the same fake-review signals worth checking regardless of the hateful language itself. If both apply, cite both grounds in your report rather than relying on just one, since a report covering multiple applicable violations is generally stronger than one resting on a single category.

How to report it effectively

Flag the review citing the specific hate speech or policy-violation category rather than a general complaint, Google's moderation teams can act on a clear policy violation faster than an ambiguous dispute about accuracy or tone. Quote the specific language in your report rather than just describing it generally, since specificity helps a moderator evaluate the violation without having to guess at exactly what was said.

When it crosses into harassment

Hateful language directed repeatedly at specific named staff, rather than the business broadly, can escalate into targeted harassment, with its own more urgent reporting path separate from a standard review dispute. Treat a pattern of hateful content aimed at an individual employee as the more serious problem it actually is, not just another bad review to flag and move past.

Don't let it sit unreported

Hate speech violations tend to get removed relatively reliably once properly flagged, since they're one of the clearer-cut categories platforms actively want off their services, but that removal doesn't happen automatically. File the report promptly rather than assuming the content will be caught by automated moderation on its own.

What to tell staff if they see it first

Employees are often the first to spot this kind of review, since they check listings more frequently than owners do. Give staff a simple instruction: screenshot it, flag it to whoever handles reporting, and don't engage with it publicly themselves, even with good intentions. A well-meaning but heated staff reply can turn a clear-cut violation into a messier public exchange that distracts from the straightforward report that should have been filed instead.
