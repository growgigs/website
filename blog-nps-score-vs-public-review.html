What's the Difference Between an NPS Survey Score and a Public Review?

GrowGigs Team

A Net Promoter Score survey asks a customer one simple question, usually right after a purchase or support interaction, and reduces their answer to a number your team tracks over time. A public review asks nothing and waits for whoever feels strongly enough to write one. Both are forms of customer feedback, but they measure different things, reach different audiences, and a good score on one tells you very little about the other.

What each one actually measures

NPS asks a narrow, specific question, how likely are you to recommend us, on a 0-10 scale, and converts the aggregate answer into a single tracking number your business watches trend up or down over time. A public review has no fixed question at all, a customer writes about whatever stood out to them, a specific staff member, a billing issue, a long wait, which means it captures texture and detail that a single numeric score never will.

Who sees each one is the bigger practical difference

An NPS score and the comments attached to it typically stay internal, visible to your team for tracking and improvement, not published anywhere a prospective customer would see them. A public review is the opposite by design, posted directly to a platform like Google or Yelp specifically so other potential customers can read it before deciding whether to do business with you.

Why a strong NPS and bad public reviews can coexist

  • Different respondent pools. NPS surveys usually go to customers who completed a transaction and engaged enough to respond, while public reviews skew toward people with either an unusually great or unusually bad experience, the quieter middle rarely bothers to post either way.
  • Different timing. An NPS survey often fires right after a purchase, capturing a snapshot in time, while a public review can be posted weeks or months later, sometimes after a separate issue the survey never touched.
  • Different stakes for the respondent. Answering an internal survey carries no social weight, while posting a public review is a visible act some customers reserve for when they feel strongly enough to put their name on it.

Why treating a dip in NPS like a bad review is a mistake

A declining NPS trend is a signal to investigate internally, look at recent operational changes, staffing, or process shifts that might explain the dip, not something to respond to publicly since no one outside your business sees the number in the first place. Conflating the two means either over-reacting publicly to a private metric, or under-reacting to an actual public review because your team is focused on the survey dashboard instead of what's visible on your profile right now.

Where the two genuinely connect

A customer who gives you a low NPS score has sometimes already posted, or is about to post, a matching public review, which makes a low-scoring survey response worth a quick look at your public profiles as a precaution, not because the two are the same signal, but because they can originate from the same underlying frustration. If that frustration is a fake or policy-violating claim rather than a legitimate complaint, that's a reporting question, not a survey-response question.

Using both without confusing their purpose

NPS works best as a longitudinal internal health check, tracked consistently over quarters to catch trends before they become visible publicly. Public reviews work best as a real-time, customer-facing signal that needs its own direct response, a reply when it's legitimate feedback, a platform report when it violates policy, handled on its own terms rather than folded into whatever the latest survey dashboard happens to show.
