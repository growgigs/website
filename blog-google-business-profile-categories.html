Google Business Profile Categories: How to Pick the Right Ones

GrowGigs Team

Category selection is one of the most consequential, and most overlooked, parts of setting up a Google Business Profile. Here's how to approach it.

Primary category carries the most weight

Your primary category is the single strongest signal Google uses to understand what your business does and which searches it should appear for. Choosing a category that's too broad, "Restaurant" instead of "Italian Restaurant", or slightly off from your actual core service means you're competing in searches you're not best suited for, while missing visibility in the more specific searches you'd actually win.

Be as specific as the options allow

Google's category list is extensive and often has a far more precise option than the generic one businesses default to. A plumber who specializes in drain cleaning has a specific category available rather than just "Plumber." The more precisely a category matches what you actually do, the better positioned you are for the exact searches your real customers run.

Secondary categories round out the picture

You can add secondary categories for other services you genuinely offer, these help you show up for a wider range of relevant searches without diluting your primary category. The key word is genuinely, adding a long list of secondary categories for services you don't actually provide, hoping to capture more searches, tends to backfire. It violates guidelines and can trigger the same kind of suspension issues covered elsewhere on this blog.

Match categories to what you actually do, not what you wish ranked for

It's tempting to pick a high-volume search category even when it's not quite accurate, but Google's systems and other users can usually tell when a category doesn't match the actual business, mismatched reviews, photos, or website content are a signal in themselves. A precise, honest category match outperforms an inflated one over time.

Review your categories periodically, not just at setup

Businesses change what they offer, add services, phase out others, and the category list itself gets updated with new, more specific options over time. A category selected correctly a few years ago might no longer be the best match, or a more precise option might now exist. Checking in on this occasionally, rather than treating it as a one-time setup task, keeps the profile aligned with both your business and the available options.

How many secondary categories is too many

There's no official cap that determines when a profile looks suspicious, but a useful gut check is this: could you defend every single category on the list to someone looking at your actual website and services? If a secondary category describes something you technically could do but don't actively offer or advertise, it's probably doing more harm than good, it dilutes the clarity of what you're actually known for without meaningfully expanding real visibility.

What this looks like for a multi-service business

A business genuinely offering several distinct services, say, a company doing both general contracting and specialized flooring installation, benefits from an honest primary/secondary split: primary category reflecting the larger or more defining part of the business, secondary categories covering the other genuine services. This is different from a single-service business listing a dozen loosely related categories to cast a wider net, the former is accurate breadth, the latter is dilution.

The practical approach

Start by searching Google's category list for your core service using the most specific language you'd use to describe it, not the generic industry term. Pick the closest accurate match as primary. Add secondary categories only for services you genuinely, currently provide. Then revisit the choice every so often rather than setting it once and forgetting about it.
