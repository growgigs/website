How Reviews Affect Your Local Search Ranking

GrowGigs Team

Reviews get talked about as if they're the whole game in local SEO. They're not, but they're a real, documented factor, and understanding how they actually connect to ranking helps you focus effort in the right place.

Review signals Google actually weighs

Google's own guidance on local ranking names review count and score among the factors that feed into local search and maps results, alongside relevance and proximity. It's not a single "more reviews equals higher rank" formula, it's one input working alongside category accuracy, proximity to the searcher, and your profile's overall completeness.

Volume matters, but so does recency

A business with two hundred reviews from three years ago and nothing since sends a different signal than one steadily accumulating reviews month over month. Recency suggests an actively operating, engaged business, which is part of why a dormant review section can hold back an otherwise solid profile even with a high total count.

Rating matters, within reason

A very low average rating is a genuine disadvantage, both for ranking signals and, more directly, for whether searchers click through and choose you once you do appear. That said, a perfect 5.0 isn't required, and a healthy mix of reviews (including some criticism) often reads as more credible than an unnaturally flawless record.

Review text itself can matter for relevance

Reviews that naturally mention your services, products, or location can reinforce relevance signals for related searches, this isn't something you can engineer by asking customers to stuff keywords into their reviews (and doing so risks looking manipulative), but it's part of why organic, varied review content tends to help more than a handful of generic five-star ratings.

What reviews don't override

A business with excellent reviews but an incomplete or unverified profile, wrong category, or significant distance from the searcher, will still struggle to rank well. Reviews strengthen a foundation, they don't replace one. If your profile has structural gaps, fixing those usually matters more than chasing additional reviews first.

The practical takeaway

Focus on genuinely earning a steady, ongoing stream of reviews rather than a one-time push, and respond to what comes in. That consistent pattern, paired with a complete, accurate profile, is what the evidence actually supports, not any specific review count you need to hit.
