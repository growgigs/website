How Agencies Can Offer Review Removal Without Building It In-House

GrowGigs Team

Clients ask their marketing or reputation agency to "just make the bad review go away" more often than most agencies are set up to handle. Building that capability internally is a real investment; subcontracting it under your own brand is usually the faster, lower-risk path to actually offering it.

Why most agencies don't build this in-house

Review removal isn't a skill that overlaps naturally with the rest of agency work like SEO, ads, or social. It requires knowing the specific policies each platform enforces, tracking how those policies shift, and running a process for evidence-gathering and follow-up that doesn't resemble anything else on a typical agency's plate. For most agencies, hiring or training for this one narrow function doesn't pencil out against the volume of cases they'd actually handle.

What white-label subcontracting actually looks like

Your agency stays the client-facing relationship. A specialized partner does the actual reporting work, under your branding on anything the client sees, reporting results back to you, which you then relay (or the partner relays directly under your name, depending on the arrangement). The client experiences one relationship, with your agency; the mechanics of who's actually filing the reports stay behind the scenes.

Where this fits into an existing service lineup

  • As an add-on to reputation management. If you already offer review generation or response management, removal fills the gap for the reviews that shouldn't be responded to at all, they should be gone.
  • As a retention tool. A client dealing with a damaging fake review is often looking for someone to solve it right now, being able to say yes rather than referring them elsewhere keeps that relationship with you.
  • As a standalone offer. Some agencies bring in new clients specifically around a reputation crisis, then expand into other services once that immediate problem is handled.

What to look for in a subcontracting partner

Billing structure matters most, a partner paid on confirmed results rather than a flat retainer keeps your margin predictable and your incentives aligned with the client's. Beyond that: real white-label reporting (not obviously branded by someone else), honest communication about what doesn't qualify for removal rather than blanket promises, and a partner who explains their process clearly enough that you can explain it to clients with confidence.

Margin and pricing considerations

Since you're paying a partner per result, your client pricing needs enough margin above that cost to make it worthwhile, while staying competitive with what a client could find by searching directly. Many agencies price this as a flat per-review or per-case fee to clients, keeping the underlying per-result cost structure simple to manage internally.

Getting started without overcommitting

You don't need to announce a new service line to start. Handling it quietly for a client who's already asking is the lowest-risk way to test a partner's process and results before deciding whether to formalize it as a standard offering.
