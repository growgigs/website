Can a Review Be Removed If the Poster Violated the Platform's Own Minimum-Age Policy?

GrowGigs Team

Most review platforms, like most online services, set a minimum age for creating an account, typically 13, buried in terms of service almost nobody reads. It's a legitimate, if narrow, removal ground when you can actually establish the poster was below that threshold, though establishing it in practice is harder than the policy itself might suggest.

Why this is its own distinct policy ground

Google, Trustpilot, and most comparable platforms prohibit account creation below a set minimum age as a condition of using the service at all, independent of anything the review itself says. This differs from an account-compromise case, where the account holder is a legitimate adult whose account was misused, the violation here is about who's actually eligible to hold the account in the first place, not who's currently controlling it.

What you'd actually need to establish this

  • Direct evidence of the poster's age, a social media profile, a school enrollment detail, or another public source clearly indicating they're below the platform's minimum.
  • A credible connection between that identity and the specific review, not just evidence someone underage exists somewhere, but that this is actually the same person who posted.
  • Independent corroboration where possible, a single ambiguous data point is a weak foundation for a report that's ultimately asking a platform to act on someone's personal information.

Why this ground is used rarely, and that's appropriate

Age is genuinely difficult to verify from outside, and platforms are understandably cautious about actioning reports built on assumptions about someone's age rather than solid evidence, since an incorrect accusation here touches on sensitive territory most moderation teams want to avoid getting wrong. Reserve this report for cases where the evidence is specific and reasonably clear, not for a general hunch that a review "sounds like it was written by a kid."

This is different from a fake-name or impersonation case

A reviewer using a fake name is a separate violation, misrepresenting identity, that doesn't depend on age at all, an adult can just as easily use a fake name as a minor can. Cite the specific ground that actually applies to your situation, age violation or fake identity, rather than blending them together in a single vague report that makes it harder for a moderator to evaluate either claim properly.

How to file this report if you genuinely have the evidence

Present the specific evidence establishing both the poster's age and their connection to the review, rather than asserting the conclusion without showing the underlying basis for it. Cite the platform's own terms-of-service age requirement directly in your report, since this policy ground is less commonly invoked than most and a moderator benefits from being pointed to the exact provision you're relying on.

Weigh whether this is actually your strongest available ground

If the same review also has other, more straightforward violations available, off-topic content, a non-customer posting, policy violations that don't require establishing someone's age at all, those grounds are usually easier to prove and faster to act on. Save the age-policy report for situations where it's genuinely the strongest, or only, violation that actually applies.

Think carefully before publicly identifying a minor

Even when you've established a credible age violation, avoid naming or publicly identifying the individual in any public response or social post referencing the situation, since that carries its own real risks regardless of how the underlying review dispute resolves. Keep the evidence and the report itself private, directed only at the platform's moderation process, rather than treated as material for a public response to the review in question, since that restraint protects your business just as much as it protects the person involved.
