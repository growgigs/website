How Many Google Reviews Do You Need to Rank Locally?

GrowGigs Team

People ask this expecting a number, 50, 100, whatever it is this year. There isn't one. Google has never published a review count threshold, and any specific figure you see quoted is someone's observation from their own market, not a rule that applies to yours.

Why there's no fixed number

Local ranking depends on your specific competitive landscape, category, and location, all at once. A dentist in a small town might rank well with 15 reviews if competitors have 10, while a plumber in a major metro area might need well over 100 to compete with established players who've been collecting reviews for a decade. The "right" number is relative to what your actual competitors have, not a universal benchmark.

What matters more than the total count

  • Relative to competitors, not absolute. Check what the businesses currently ranking above you actually have, that's a far more useful benchmark than an arbitrary number.
  • Recency. A steady trickle of new reviews signals an active business; a stagnant total from years ago doesn't carry the same weight even if the number itself is high.
  • Rating alongside count. A large volume of mediocre reviews doesn't outperform a smaller volume of strong ones as cleanly as people assume.

The trap of chasing a number

Businesses that fixate on hitting a specific review count sometimes resort to review-gating, incentivizing, or other tactics that violate platform policies, risking exactly the kind of enforcement action that removes reviews or restricts the profile. The count isn't worth pursuing at the expense of doing it legitimately.

A more useful way to think about it

Instead of a target number, aim for a consistent, ongoing collection process, asking satisfied customers at the right moment, making it easy with a direct link, and doing it as a regular habit rather than a one-time campaign. Over months, that consistency naturally builds a review profile competitive for your specific market, without needing to guess at a number in advance.

When review count genuinely isn't your bottleneck

If your profile is incomplete, your category is wrong, or your NAP information is inconsistent across the web, more reviews won't fix those underlying issues. It's worth confirming the fundamentals are solid before assuming review volume is what's holding your ranking back.
