What Insurance Should a White-Label Partner Carry?

GrowGigs Team

An agency vetting a white-label partner usually asks about pricing, SLAs, and track record, and rarely thinks to ask what insurance the partner actually carries, until a client dispute or a data mishap turns that oversight into a genuinely expensive problem. A white-label partner's execution failure can become your liability, since your client's contract is with you, not with the vendor working quietly behind the scenes.

Why this question belongs on your vetting checklist

Your client's contract is with your agency, not with whichever partner is actually performing the work behind the scenes, which means if that partner makes a costly mistake, your agency is the one facing the client's complaint and potentially their legal claim. A partner's insurance coverage is effectively a backstop for your own exposure, not just a detail about their internal risk management that has nothing to do with you.

Professional liability, or errors and omissions, is the core coverage to confirm

  • E&O insurance covers claims arising from mistakes in professional services, a botched removal request that damages a client relationship, or advice that leads to a measurable loss, the kind of claim that can actually arise from reputation management or local SEO work.
  • General liability is a different, narrower coverage focused on physical injury or property damage, and doesn't substitute for E&O when the actual risk is professional error rather than a physical incident.
  • Cyber liability matters if the partner handles client data directly, account access, personal information, anything that could trigger a breach notification obligation if mishandled.

Don't just take a partner's word for it

Request an actual certificate of insurance, not just a verbal confirmation that coverage exists, since a certificate names the specific policy type, coverage limits, and the insurer, all details a casual assurance can't verify. A legitimate partner should have no hesitation producing this document, and genuine reluctance to share it is itself a meaningful signal worth weighing heavily in your decision. Keep a copy of that certificate on file alongside your signed partnership agreement, since you'll want it readily available if a client dispute ever actually requires you to reference it.

Coverage limits matter as much as coverage existing at all

A partner carrying the bare legal minimum E&O coverage offers far less real protection than one carrying limits proportional to the actual client relationships and revenue flowing through the partnership. Match your expectations to the actual scale of the engagement, a partner handling a handful of small local clients needs less coverage than one handling your agency's largest, highest-visibility accounts. A policy limit that looked reasonable when the partnership started can quietly become inadequate as the relationship grows and the stakes of any single mistake grow along with it.

Ask how coverage interacts with your own contract terms

This connects directly to the SLA and guarantee terms already worth nailing down in a white-label contract, since insurance and SLA remedies address different failure scenarios and work best when they're coordinated rather than treated as entirely separate conversations. An SLA might cover a missed deadline, while insurance covers the deeper financial fallout from an actual professional error, and a well-structured partnership agreement accounts for both.

Revisit this periodically, not just at onboarding

A partner's insurance coverage can lapse, get downgraded, or simply expire without you ever being notified, the same way a partner's track record deserves periodic re-evaluation rather than a one-time check at the start of the relationship. Build a periodic certificate refresh into your ongoing partner management, not just into your initial due diligence before signing. Set a recurring reminder tied to the policy's renewal date, since relying on memory alone for something that only matters once a problem actually surfaces is exactly how this kind of gap goes unnoticed for years.
