How Many Bad Reviews Does It Take to Hurt Your Business?

GrowGigs Team

There's no universal number, and anyone who gives you one specific figure is guessing. What actually matters is a handful of factors that determine whether a few bad reviews are a non-issue or a real problem.

The math changes with your review count

A single one-star review has a very different effect on a business with 8 total reviews than on one with 800. Early on, every review moves your average rating significantly, and a prospective customer scanning a thin review list will read every single one. Once you've got a large volume of reviews, a handful of negative ones have far less mathematical impact on your overall average, though they can still be prominent if they're the most recent ones shown.

Recency matters more than total count

Most platforms surface recent reviews first by default. A business with an excellent long-term track record but two recent one-star reviews can look worse at a glance than the numbers actually justify, because those two are what a new visitor sees immediately. This is part of why staying on top of new reviews, not just your all-time average, matters.

What the reviews actually say matters as much as the star count

A cluster of reviews mentioning the same specific issue, slow response times, a particular product, a recurring service problem, reads as a pattern and carries more weight with a prospective customer than scattered, unrelated one-off complaints. Content, not just star rating, shapes how much a review actually influences a decision.

Your industry changes the stakes

Trust-dependent categories, medical practices, law firms, contractors, anything involving a significant spend or personal risk, tend to be more sensitive to visible negative reviews than lower-stakes, high-frequency purchase categories. The same handful of bad reviews carries different weight depending on what you're asking someone to trust you with.

What actually helps, regardless of the number

  • Remove what shouldn't be there. Fake reviews, non-customer reviews, and policy violations, cleared out rather than left to dilute your average.
  • Respond to the reviews that are genuine. A thoughtful public response can offset a negative review's impact even when it can't be removed.
  • Keep earning new reviews. A steady flow of recent positive reviews naturally pushes older negative ones down and out of first view.

The honest answer

It's not about a magic number of bad reviews, it's about your current volume, how recent the negative ones are, whether they cluster around a real pattern, and how trust-sensitive your industry is. Two of those factors, removing what shouldn't be there and staying current, are things you can actually control.
