Can You Get a Fake Listing for Your Business Removed From a Local Business Directory Site?

GrowGigs Team

A search for your own business name turns up a listing on a directory site you've never heard of, wrong hours, an outdated phone number, maybe a review section with content you didn't ask for. Most of these aren't malicious, they're auto-generated from scraped public data, but they're still live, often outdated, and sometimes more visible in search than you'd like.

Why these listings exist in the first place

Yellow Pages-style directories and local-data aggregators build much of their inventory by pulling from public records, business registrations, and other directories, generating a page automatically whether or not the business ever signed up. This is different from Google's duplicate Maps listing issue or a dedicated scam site built to impersonate you, here the listing is usually just a byproduct of automated data aggregation, not a targeted act against your business specifically.

Claiming vs. removing: two different goals

  • Claiming the listing gives you control over the information displayed, correcting hours, phone number, and category, which is often the faster, more useful outcome than removal.
  • Requesting removal makes sense when the directory itself has little traffic or relevance to your business, and you'd rather it simply disappear from search results tied to your name than maintain yet another profile.

How to actually request removal

Most directories bury an "opt out," "remove my business," or "claim this listing" link somewhere in the page footer or within the listing itself, since they're required to offer it under most data-privacy frameworks even when the listing was auto-generated. If you can't find one, the site's general contact or support page is the fallback, specify the exact listing URL and ask for removal citing inaccurate or unauthorized data, which tends to get a faster response than a vague request.

Why claiming is usually the better default

An unclaimed directory listing with outdated hours or an old phone number is actively worse for customers than no listing at all, someone finds your business, gets wrong information, and the experience reflects on you even though the directory built the page without your input. Claiming the handful of directories that actually rank for your business name resolves that risk directly, rather than playing whack-a-mole trying to remove every auto-generated page that exists somewhere on the internet.

When it's worth escalating beyond a standard opt-out request

If a directory ignores a removal or correction request entirely, or if the listing includes content that goes beyond basic business data, fabricated reviews, incorrect ownership information, that's a stronger case worth a follow-up citing the specific inaccuracy directly rather than a generic opt-out. This is a lighter-weight version of the scam website impersonation process already covered, most legitimate directories will correct course once pressed; a site built purely to deceive requires the fuller takedown approach.

Keeping ahead of new auto-generated listings

New directories periodically scrape the same public records that generated the original listing, which means claiming or removing one doesn't guarantee a similar page won't appear elsewhere later. Checking your business name in search every few months and handling new listings as they surface is a more realistic approach than trying to preempt every directory that might eventually pull your information.

Why a review section on an unclaimed listing is a separate concern

Some aggregator directories display their own review widget, sometimes pulling in reviews from other platforms, sometimes allowing visitors to post directly on the directory itself, independent of anything happening on your Google or Trustpilot profile. If that review section shows content you can't account for, especially anything that looks fabricated or duplicated from elsewhere, treat it as its own issue requiring the directory's specific reporting process, separate from the general listing-accuracy request covering hours and contact details.

Deciding which directories are actually worth the effort

Not every auto-generated listing deserves equal attention, a directory that ranks on page one for your business name and gets real traffic is worth claiming properly, while an obscure aggregator buried deep in search results with no visible traffic may not justify the time either way. Prioritizing the handful that actually show up when someone searches your business name is a more efficient use of effort than treating every scraped listing on the internet as equally urgent.
