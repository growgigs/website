How Do GBP "Services" and "Products" Sections Affect Local Ranking?

GrowGigs Team

Most owners fill out their Google Business Profile's category and hours, then leave the Services and Products sections half-empty or entirely blank, treating them as optional decoration rather than active listing content. Those two sections do real work, both for how your listing gets matched to searches and for what a customer actually sees before they decide to call.

What these sections actually are

The Services section lets you list specific offerings with names, descriptions, and optional pricing, while Products works similarly for physical items a business sells. Both sit below your primary category and attributes on the profile, visible to anyone who views your listing directly, and both feed into Google's broader understanding of exactly what your business does.

Why this matters for ranking, specifically

  • Specific service names help match specific searches. A generic "plumbing" category paired with explicit service entries like "water heater installation" or "drain cleaning" gives Google more precise text to match against a searcher's exact query.
  • Detailed descriptions add relevant keyword context without reading as keyword stuffing, since the content genuinely describes a real offering rather than existing purely to game a ranking signal.
  • An empty or sparse section is a missed signal, not a neutral one. Competitors who've filled theirs out thoroughly are giving Google more to work with for the same category of search.

This is distinct from the attributes feature

Attributes answer yes/no or checklist-style questions, wheelchair accessible, free Wi-Fi, outdoor seating, the kind of binary facts covered in a separate post on getting attributes right. Services and Products are open-ended listing content you write yourself, closer to a condensed menu or service catalog than a checkbox, and they carry their own distinct weight in how your profile gets matched to search intent.

Keep entries specific and genuinely accurate

Vague service names like "general services" or "miscellaneous" do essentially nothing for matching, while a name and description that actually reflects what you do, written the way a real customer would search for it, carries real value. Resist the temptation to list services you don't actually offer just to capture more search terms, since a customer who books based on an inaccurate listing becomes a dissatisfied customer almost immediately, and that dissatisfaction tends to show up in your reviews before long.

Pricing is optional, but worth considering

You can add a price or price range to each service entry, which some businesses skip out of hesitation about committing to a number publicly. A visible price range can actually reduce low-intent inquiries from searchers who were never going to be a good fit on budget, while a listing with no pricing information at all leaves that filtering work entirely to the phone call or message instead.

Review and update these sections periodically

Services change, a seasonal offering ends, a new one launches, pricing shifts, and a Services section that hasn't been touched in two years is quietly drifting out of sync with what you actually do. Treat it the same way you'd treat any other piece of active marketing content, something to revisit periodically rather than fill out once at setup and never look at again.

Don't treat this as a one-time setup task

Businesses often fill out Services and Products thoroughly when they first claim their profile, then genuinely forget the sections exist once the initial setup is done. Put a recurring reminder on your calendar, quarterly is reasonable for most businesses, to revisit both sections alongside your attributes and hours, since a listing that's actively maintained across every section tends to outperform one that was only ever touched once, however thorough that original setup might have been.
