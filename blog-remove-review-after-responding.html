Can You Remove a Google Review After Responding to It?

GrowGigs Team

Business owners often assume that once they've publicly replied to a review, they've lost the right to challenge it. That's not quite how it works. Here's what your response actually does and doesn't affect.

Your reply doesn't change the review's eligibility

Google evaluates a review against its content policies based on the review itself, who posted it, and how, not on whether or how you responded. Replying doesn't forfeit your ability to later flag the review if you discover grounds for removal, and it doesn't strengthen your case either. The two are functionally separate.

Where your reply actually matters

A public reply can matter in a practical sense, though, mostly around what it reveals. If your response acknowledges the reviewer as a real customer ("I'm sorry your visit on the 12th didn't go well"), that can undercut a later claim that the reviewer was never a customer. This isn't a formal rule Google enforces, it's just that a removal report claiming "this person never visited us" reads inconsistently next to your own public reply confirming they did.

What this means practically

  • If you suspect a review might not be genuine, hold off on a specific reply. A generic, non-committal response ("Thanks for your feedback, please reach out directly") keeps your options open while you look into it.
  • Once you've confirmed a review is fake or ineligible, you can still respond publicly. Nothing stops you from replying and also filing a removal report at the same time.
  • If you've already replied and confirmed details that undercut a removal case, it's still worth trying. A slightly weaker case is still better than none, but be honest with yourself about what the report can actually claim.

Can you edit or delete your own reply?

Yes. Business owners can edit or remove their own replies to reviews at any time through their Google Business Profile. This doesn't retroactively change anything about the review's removal eligibility, but it's useful to know if an earlier reply no longer reflects where things stand.

The bottom line

Responding to a review and pursuing its removal aren't mutually exclusive, but what you say in a public reply can either support or undercut a later removal argument, so it's worth a moment's thought before you hit post, especially on a review you're not yet sure about.
