Signs a Google Review Is Fake

GrowGigs Team

Before you spend time building a removal report, it's worth knowing what actually points to a fake review versus a real one you just don't like. Here's what to look for.

The reviewer profile itself

  • No photo, generic name, zero history. A brand-new account with one review, ever, is a common pattern, though not proof on its own.
  • A pattern of one-off reviews. Check their profile: several single reviews left for unrelated businesses, often clustered in a short window, is one of the strongest signals available.
  • Reviews for direct competitors. If the same account also reviewed a business that directly competes with you, that's worth flagging as a possible conflict of interest, not just a fake-engagement report.

The content of the review

  • Details that don't match your business. Wrong service, wrong location, a product you don't sell, staff names that don't exist.
  • Generic, could-apply-to-anyone language. "Terrible service, would not recommend" with nothing specific is common in mass-produced fake reviews, though plenty of real reviewers are also brief.
  • Posted in a cluster. Several similar low-star reviews landing within hours or a couple of days of each other, especially if your business hasn't had a review in a while, is a pattern worth investigating.

The timing

Cross-check the review's date against your actual records. A review dated for a day you were closed, before you opened, or referencing a visit that doesn't match any transaction is one of the more concrete pieces of evidence you can put in a removal report, since it's a fact Google can verify against, not just your word.

What isn't a reliable sign on its own

A low star rating alone means nothing, negative doesn't equal fake. Short reviews aren't automatically fake either, plenty of real customers write one line. Treat any single signal above as a starting point, not a verdict, until two or three line up together.

What to do once you've spotted one

Screenshot everything first (the review, the date, the reviewer's profile and their other reviews) before it can be edited or deleted. Then flag it through your Google Business Profile with the specific reason that matches what you found, and include the evidence rather than just asserting the review is fake.
