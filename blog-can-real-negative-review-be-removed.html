Can You Remove a Real Negative Google Review?

GrowGigs Team

The assumption most business owners start with is that a genuine review, even a harsh or unfair one, is permanent. That's not quite right. A real review can still violate Google's policies depending on its content and how it was posted, it just takes more digging than an obviously fake one does.

"Real" and "eligible for removal" aren't opposites

Google doesn't remove reviews because they're negative. It removes reviews that violate its content policies, and a review from an actual customer can still cross those lines. The reviewer being real doesn't mean the review itself is exempt.

Cases where a genuine review can still come down

  • It contains prohibited content. Hate speech, harassment, obscene language, or personal attacks unrelated to the actual experience.
  • It's off-topic. Complaints about pricing policy, staff turnover, or unrelated grievances that aren't about an actual transaction or visit.
  • It discloses private information. Reviews that name and target employees personally, or share private details, can violate Google's policy even from a real customer.
  • It was posted as leverage. A review explicitly used to extort a refund, discount, or other concession ("remove my bad review if you...") violates Google's policy against extortion.
  • The account behind it is compromised or repurposed. Sometimes a review reads as genuine but the account posting it shows signs of being bought, sold, or hijacked, which is worth investigating rather than assuming.

Cases where it won't come down, and why that's fair

A review that's simply negative but accurately describes a real experience, slow service, a product that didn't meet expectations, a bad interaction, isn't going anywhere through a removal request. Google's system is built to protect that kind of feedback, and it should be. The honest move here isn't removal, it's responding professionally and, if it's warranted, making it right with the customer.

How to actually check

Before assuming a review is untouchable, look at three things: the review's exact wording against Google's policy categories, the reviewer's account history and activity pattern, and the actual context of how and when it was posted. Reviews that look identical on the surface can land in completely different outcomes once you dig into those details, this is usually where a case-by-case review does more than a single skim.
