Can a Review Be Removed If It Was Posted to the Wrong Platform Entirely?

GrowGigs Team

A customer meant to leave feedback through a private support channel, or meant to post on a different platform altogether, and the complaint ends up sitting on your Google profile instead, word for word describing an experience that doesn't match anything in your own records. This is a narrower, stranger problem than a fake or non-customer review, and it needs a slightly different approach to sort out.

What "wrong platform" actually looks like in practice

The most common version is a customer who genuinely meant to complain somewhere else, a company's internal support ticket system, a different platform entirely, and accidentally searched for and posted on your Google listing instead, sometimes because your business name is similar to the one they intended to review. The content of the review describes a real experience, it's just not an experience with your business at all.

Why this differs from the standard non-customer policy

A typical non-customer review, the kind covered in the non-customer removal post already published, usually involves someone with no transaction record attacking a business they're at least aware of. A wrong-platform mismatch is different, the reviewer often has a completely genuine grievance, just against an unrelated business, and may not even realize they posted it to your listing at all. The policy violation (reviewing a business you never interacted with) is technically the same, but the reviewer's intent is honest confusion rather than malice.

How to confirm it's actually a mismatch

  • Check your own records first. No matching transaction, name, date, or location tied to your business is the first signal.
  • Look for details that point elsewhere. A mentioned city, product, or staff name that has nothing to do with your business is strong evidence of a mix-up rather than a dishonest complaint about your business specifically.
  • Consider name similarity. If a business with a very similar name exists nearby or online, that's often the explanation, and worth noting directly in your report.

How to report it

Google's standard reporting flow lets you flag a review as not relating to your business, select that category rather than a defamation or policy-violation report, since this isn't really about the review being false, it's about it being posted against the wrong entity entirely. Including the specific detail that points to the correct (different) business strengthens the report considerably, giving the reviewing team something concrete to verify.

What to do if the reviewer realizes their mistake first

Some reviewers notice the error themselves, sometimes after you respond professionally noting that you don't have a record of their visit, and will delete or correct the post without needing a formal removal process at all. A calm public reply asking if they meant a different location or business, rather than an accusatory one, often resolves this faster and with less friction than waiting on a platform report.

Why similar business names make this more common than it sounds

Franchises, chains with multiple locations under slightly different naming conventions, and businesses that happen to share a name with an unrelated company in another city are all more prone to this kind of mismatch than a business with a genuinely unique name. If your business falls into one of these categories, it's worth periodically searching your own name to see what else comes up in results, since that same name confusion affecting customers can also work in reverse against you.

What this doesn't excuse

A reviewer claiming the wrong-platform explanation after being confronted about a review that actually does describe your business accurately is a different situation entirely, and shouldn't be taken at face value just because the explanation sounds plausible. This distinction matters when deciding whether to pursue a formal report, the evidence (no matching transaction, details pointing elsewhere) needs to actually support the mismatch claim, not just the reviewer's say-so after the fact.
